## 1. Which coordination patterns are in common use, and for each: who uses it, how it works, effort for a non-programmer, strengths, weaknesses and examples?

### Takeaway
All ten patterns exist, but for one person or a small team the real-world evidence clusters on the low-tech ones: the human as router, a shared set of files or a shared store that every assistant reads at the start of a session, and an existing task tracker; agent-to-agent messaging, frameworks and gateways are mostly developer territory. The single most consequential fact for this reader: ChatGPT custom GPTs are scheduled to retire on 2026-12-11 and their custom Actions do not migrate, so a hub exposed as a remote MCP server is now the common denominator across ChatGPT, Claude, Microsoft 365 Copilot and Grok (Microsoft 365 Copilot also accepts a REST API with an OpenAPI description), with plan and admin limits that differ per vendor.

### Cited Findings

#### 1.0 Baseline: using several assistants and carrying context by hand is normal
- [DOC][VENDOR] Glean's Work AI Index 2026 (6,000 full-time digital workers in the US, UK and Australia, fielded Dec 2025 to Jan 2026): 77% of AI users juggle multiple AI tools every week and 33% use four or more; workers spend 2.3 hours a week "feeding AI context" (14% of their AI time); 60% rerun the same prompt across multiple tools; 36% of AI sessions "fail" outright; only 0.5% of Claude users use Claude alone. — [Glean Work AI Index](https://glean.com/work-ai-institute/reports/work-ai-index) (publication date not shown; fieldwork Dec 2025–Jan 2026)
- [DOC] BCG survey of 300 finance, legal and tax professionals (fielded Dec 2025): "In 2025, professionals regularly used an average of three different tools, versus just over two in 2024"; ChatGPT "remains the most widely used and liked". — [BCG](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/as-foundational-ai-improves-providers-can-thrive) (2026-04-23)
- [DOC][VENDOR] Salesforce/YouGov (1,207 US adults, 517 employed, fielded 4–5 Dec 2025): workers who use AI both inside and outside work systems "toggle between an average of four different AI options"; 76% say their preferred AI tools lack access to company data or work context. — [Salesforce newsroom](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/ai-tools-lack-job-context/) (2026-01-08)
- [DOC][VENDOR][OLD] ClickUp survey of "over 1,000" knowledge workers (June 2025): 46.5% must move between two or more AI tools to complete a single task (start in one tool, copy results to another, reformulate prompts, reconcile outputs); 34.4% use AI tools with zero integration into their projects, documents and chats. — [ClickUp](https://clickup.com/blog/ai-sprawl-survey/) (2025-07-15)

#### Pattern 1 — The human as router (copy and paste, hand-off prompts)
- [ANEC][VENDOR] How it is described: "This is context switching, and right now most of us are handling it with the clipboard." — [HackMD blog](https://hackmd.io/blog/2026/07/23/context-switching-2026) (2026-07-23)
- [ANEC] Hacker News thread on making Claude Code and Codex talk through Git: several commenters say they relay by hand ("I'm the intermediary though"). — [Hacker News item 48345837](https://hn.svelte.dev/item/48345837) (about June 2026; page showed "5 months ago")
- [ANEC][VENDOR] Cost of routing by hand, one developer's account: he kept a separate "AI briefing doc" to paste into new chats and "By 10 AM, I'd already spent more time explaining my own work to machines than actually doing the work"; he estimates 15–20 minutes a day saved after moving to a shared memory (he sells that product). — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/alfredoizjr/i-use-3-ai-coding-tools-every-day-heres-how-i-keep-them-in-sync-4le5) (2026-04-21, year inferred)
- [ANEC] Hand-off document beats full transcript: one user moved from clipping whole conversations ("you will burn an enormous amount of context with unnecessary tokens") to asking the first assistant to write a "markdown artifact for handoff"; a conversation of about 25,000 tokens became an artifact of about 2,800 tokens. — [personal blog](https://novistopheles.micro.blog/2026/05/14/transfer-from-chatgpt-to-claude.html) (2026-05-19)
- [DOC][OLD] Manual exports are a weak substitute for shared memory: "every memory export is just a single frozen snapshot". — [Asimov's Addendum (Substack)](https://asimovaddendum.substack.com/p/the-memory-walled-garden) (page dated 2025-10-03)
- [CASE] Even in a file-based three-vendor set-up the human stays "structural": the one who can tell the difference between "looks right" and "is right". — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/wilddog64/i-ran-three-ai-agents-from-three-different-companies-on-one-complex-project-heres-the-909) (2026-03-14, year inferred)

#### Pattern 2 — A shared memory or "second brain" exposed to every assistant (MCP or API)
- [CASE] One-folder approach by a daily user (technical background not stated): a folder of Markdown files synced to Google Drive with root instruction files (CLAUDE.md, AGENTS.md), MEMORY.md, a workspace per area of work, and Outputs and Skills folders; each assistant reads it at the start of a session; set-up "under 60 minutes". Reported problems: "Temporary decisions and notes from day-to-day work had been saved as permanent context"; different assistants interpret the folder instructions differently; scheduled cloud tasks need the computer on; she runs a periodic audit for bloat and broken routes. Part of the post is paywalled. — [AI blew my mind (Substack)](https://aiblewmymind.substack.com/p/claude-chatgpt-same-memory-context) (2026-07-30)
- [DOC] Notion as hub: Notion MCP "lets AI assistants like Claude, ChatGPT, and Cursor read from and write to your Notion pages in real-time"; "MCP tools act with your full Notion permissions. They can access everything you can access"; Notion recommends starting with "single-player use cases"; admin approval/blocking of clients is Enterprise-only. The help page does not document rate limits or how simultaneous edits are resolved. — [Notion Help](https://www.notion.com/help/notion-mcp) (no date; © 2026)
- [DOC] Obsidian as hub (open-source server, not a plugin): works directly on the `.md` files; local mode for Claude Desktop, Claude Code and Cursor; remote mode for claude.ai and other apps with remote connectors needs a small server plus a paid Obsidian Sync subscription; OAuth 2.1; read-only mode, per-tool switches and protected paths; ChatGPT is not mentioned. A user reply: "would rather scope an agent to a single folder than hand it the whole vault". — [Obsidian forum](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/vault-cortex-an-open-source-mcp-server-that-gives-ai-tools-read-and-write-access-to-your-obsidian-vault-from-any-device-without-obsidian-running/117995) (2026-09-05, updates to 2026-11-17)
- [CASE][VENDOR] One memory bank for about five clients (Claude desktop/mobile, Claude Code, a VS Code extension, Codex, an OpenClaw bot on Discord): the self-hosted memory server needed a Cloudflare Tunnel plus a roughly 250-line Cloudflare Worker to supply the OAuth 2.1 flow (dynamic client registration, PKCE) that cloud clients require; ChatGPT was not wired in; the chat apps lack lifecycle hooks, so saving memories depends on prompt instructions: "The gap is just the app surfaces, which is where most people actually spend their time." — [Hindsight blog](https://hindsight.vectorize.io/blog/2026/04/07/one-memory-for-every-ai-tool) (2026-04-07)
- [CASE] Shared memory written by several agents gets corrupted without write discipline: a solo founder's `memory.jsonl` knowledge graph suffered partial writes and duplicate entries from parallel writes; the fix was a forked server with a mutex, atomic writes and auto-repair on load; stand-up notes are pruned after 7 days while lessons and decisions are kept. — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/setas/i-run-a-solo-company-with-ai-agent-departments-50nf) (2026-03-03, year inferred)
- [ANEC] Forum experience over months: append-only hand-off files grew to about 492KB and 700KB+ in roughly six weeks and agents wasted context on stale entries (fixed by monthly rotation); "Chat-only memory gets stale quietly and becomes hard to debug"; one user tags every record as confirmed or agent guess: "Pruning keeps the store small; provenance keeps it honest". — [Cursor forum thread](https://forum.cursor.com/t/how-are-people-handling-context-across-different-ai-coding-tools/159891.md?page=2) (posts 2026-06-08 to 2026-07-25)
- [ANEC] A non-developer on the OpenAI forum reports daily use of a self-built portable memory with the same principles: targeted retrieval, separating current from historical information, and distinguishing explicit statements from AI inference. — [OpenAI community forum](https://community.openai.com/t/extended-personal-memory-for-chatgpt-chatgpt-work-codex-build-prompt-testers/1391779) (post 2026-08-22, reply 2026-09-09)
- [ANEC][SNIPPET] Several small open-source "second brain" MCP servers advertise one store for Claude, ChatGPT, Cursor and others (for example a Cloudflare Workers project and Markdown-vault projects); no independent reports of daily use were found. — [project listing](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/l1fx3gu5iz) (date not captured)

#### Pattern 3 — A shared task board where agents pick up and comment on tasks

- [DOC] GitHub Copilot cloud agent for Linear became generally available on 2026-07-23: assign the issue, the agent opens a draft pull request, will "Stream progress updates back to your Linear activity timeline" and "Request a pull request review from you when the work is complete"; needs GitHub organisation-owner and Linear workspace-admin rights and a paid Copilot plan. — [GitHub changelog](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-07-23-copilot-cloud-agent-for-linear-is-now-generally-available/) (2026-07-23)
- [DOC][OLD] Cursor in Linear: issues are assigned to the agent "the same way you already assign issues to teammates"; the session shows "the thoughts and tools, to-dos, as it progresses"; commenting on every action "can get noisy and fatigue users". — [Linear blog](https://linear.app/blog/how-cursor-integrated-with-linear-for-agents) (2025-08-21)
- [DOC] Jira: "Agents appear in the assignee field alongside human teammates"; agents can be @mentioned, triggered from workflow transitions or attached to a board column; "Every agent action is logged on the work item"; third-party agents named are GitHub Copilot, Cursor and custom agents. — [Atlassian blog](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/ai-at-work/your-jira-board-just-got-a-new-kind-of-teammate) (2026-06-18). [SNIPPET] General availability for Jira Cloud Standard, Premium and Enterprise was in May 2026 — [Atlassian community](https://community.atlassian.com/forums/discussion/3194583/introducing-agents-in-jira) (date not captured).
- [ANEC] GitHub Issues and pull requests as the board: Hacker News commenters have agents communicate "in GitHub issues and pull requests like humans do"; another coordinates sandboxed agents through Jira and Git. — [Hacker News item 48345837](https://hn.svelte.dev/item/48345837) (about June 2026)
- [CASE] A do-it-yourself board built in one day: a database with tables for agents, messages, notifications and kanban tasks; five columns (Backlog, Todo, In Progress, Review, Done); task comments with @mentions for hand-offs; a research agent that "wakes up every 30 minutes, checks for queued tasks". — [personal blog](https://maverickmolt.github.io/MaverickBlog/2026/02/03/building-mission-control) (2026-02-03)

#### Pattern 4 — A messaging channel as the bus (Slack, Teams, Discord, Telegram, WhatsApp, email)
- [DOC][VENDOR][OLD] Slack lists Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and the GitHub Copilot coding agent among the agents available in its marketplace: "Easily summon agents by @mentioning them in DMs or channels." — [Slack blog](https://slack.com/blog/developers/coding-agents-in-slack) (2025-12-11)
- [ANEC][VENDOR] Practices described by Slack's chief product officer: keep work in public channels because "Agents can only learn from what they can see" (a private channel is a blind spot for every agent); a shared triage channel where humans set priorities and an emoji reaction hands an item to an agent; agents draft, summarise and monitor, people review and decide; "Start soon, but start small." — [Anthropic/Claude blog](https://claude.com/blog/turning-conversation-into-knowledge-how-slack-builds-human-agent-teams) (2026-08-19)
- [DOC] Chat channels as the approval surface: n8n can pause an agent's tool call and send the approval request to n8n Chat, Slack, Discord, Telegram, Microsoft Teams, Gmail, WhatsApp Business Cloud, Google Chat or Outlook; the reviewer sees the tool and its parameters. — [n8n docs](https://docs.n8n.io/advanced-ai/human-in-the-loop-tools) (no date on page)
- [ANEC][SNIPPET] OpenClaw community set-ups put several bots in one Telegram supergroup or Discord server and coordinate through shared markdown files, bot-to-bot triggers and cron; a community explainer stresses that memory is not shared between agents by default; authors disagree on Telegram versus Discord. — [community kit](https://github.com/raulvidis/openclaw-multi-agent-kit) and [explainer](https://blakethom8.github.io/mobile-reports/2026-03-08_openclaw-multi-agent-explainer.html) (2026-03-08)
- [ANEC, press][SNIPPET] Email as the bus: a start-up that gives each agent its own inbox raised $6M in March 2026 and reported its user count tripling in the week OpenClaw took off; one cited use is an agent that emails you to approve something sensitive. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/10/agentmail-raises-6m-to-build-an-email-service-for-ai-agents/) (2026-03-10)
- [ANEC] Agents talking to each other directly in a channel can degenerate: commenters ask whether the exchange becomes "a never-ending snowball" and report agents that sometimes "end up hating at each other". — [Hacker News item 48345837](https://hn.svelte.dev/item/48345837) (about June 2026)

#### Pattern 5 — File-based hand-offs (markdown hand-off notes, AGENTS.md, shared folders, Git)
- [CASE] Three vendors on one project (Claude Code for planning and reviews, Codex for production code, Gemini for tests) with "no API calls, shared memory system, or orchestration framework": two files, `activeContext.md` (branch, active tasks, completion reports, lessons) and `progress.md` (done, pending, known bugs), Git as the audit trail, and every task starting from a structured spec with a required completion-report template. "The coordination problem isn't communication, it's shared state." — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/wilddog64/i-ran-three-ai-agents-from-three-different-companies-on-one-complex-project-heres-the-909) (2026-03-14, year inferred)
- [CASE][OLD] Git as the message bus with markdown files as the message format across six Claude Code sessions: what broke was vague first messages, template drift, overwritten feedback files, polling ("Humans must prompt agents to check") and a false dependency that left two agents waiting on each other until a human stepped in. — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/aviadr1/communication-protocols-for-ai-agents-that-cant-talk-to-each-other-b23) (2025-11-06)
- [ANEC] Variants reported on Hacker News: an append-only file the agents "chat via"; newline-delimited JSON logs read before and written after each task; an inbox and an outbox folder per agent with a watcher; per-role folders of markdown memos with an index of what each agent has read; a SQLite file per repository behind a local MCP tool. — [Hacker News item 48345837](https://hn.svelte.dev/item/48345837) (about June 2026)
- [ANEC] A fixed-template HANDOFF file written by the outgoing agent: "The template is the whole trick"; writing "not verified" for unchecked work reduces false status claims; instruction files drift from reality ("files drifted away from the repo") even when tools keep CLAUDE.md, AGENTS.md and .cursorrules in sync. — [Cursor forum thread](https://forum.cursor.com/t/how-are-people-handling-context-across-different-ai-coding-tools/159891.md?page=2) (2026-06-10 and 2026-07-25)
- [DOC] Controlled evidence that hand-off notes pay off: across 2,172 takeover runs, hand-offs that carry context cut the successor's median agent events by 20–59% and prompt tokens by 42–63% compared with taking over from the repository alone; gains in solved rate were smaller and model-dependent; raw traces were informative but huge (median first prompt about 87k characters versus 7–10k for notes). — [arXiv 2606.02875v2, "Handoff Debt"](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.02875) (2026-08-30)
- [ANEC][VENDOR] A shared online note as the hand-off surface between Claude Code and Codex, reached with a command-line tool rather than a bespoke integration; durable, versioned and human-readable. — [HackMD blog](https://hackmd.io/blog/2026/07/23/context-switching-2026) (2026-07-23)

#### Pattern 7 — One orchestrator agent that calls other vendors' models as workers
- [CASE] A second vendor's model as consultant, not co-worker: Claude Code hands questions it cannot verify to OpenAI's Codex through a wrapper script (the direct MCP call was hard to supervise because rate limits caused hangs). Reason: "correlated blind spots" (a different model family's mistakes "don't correlate with Claude's"). Rules: read-only sandbox, "fail-warn, never fail-stop", a one-hour lock-out file after a rate limit, and "A second opinion is a luxury. The day's work isn't." — [personal blog](https://stevekinney.com/writing/codex-as-a-second-opinion) (2026-06-04, modified 2026-10-05)
- [DOC][OLD] The orchestrator-worker pattern from the vendor that popularised it: "a lead agent coordinates the process while delegating to specialized subagents that operate in parallel"; multi-agent systems used about 15 times the tokens of a chat; each worker needs an objective, an output format, tool guidance and "clear task boundaries"; without them "one subagent explored the 2021 automotive chip crisis while 2 others duplicated work". — [Anthropic engineering blog](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system) (2025-06-13)
- [CASE] Tiering models from two vendors by task weight inside one fleet (9 agents, about 62 scheduled jobs): a cheap model for light work (about $0.05–0.10 per session), a mid model ($0.50–1.00) and a top model ($2–5); every job pins its model so a fallback bug cannot silently route cheap jobs to the expensive one. — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/sebastian_chedal/the-cost-circuit-breaker-how-we-prevent-runaway-spending-across-9-ai-agents-4i5k) (2026-04-06, year inferred)
- [ANEC][SNIPPET] "Council" set-ups send one question to several vendors' models through a single model-gateway key, have them review each other, and let a chairman model synthesise; an automation template warns that context grows as n responses plus n² evaluations, so 3–4 members is the practical limit. — [n8n template](https://n8n.io/workflows/12316-synthesize-and-compare-multiple-llm-responses-with-openrouter-council/) (date not captured)
- [ANEC] Cost caveats from Hacker News: driving one vendor's assistant from another app can be billed at API rates rather than under the subscription; one user's orchestration tool used up a $100 plan's five-hour window in about two hours while idle. — [Hacker News item 48345837](https://hn.svelte.dev/item/48345837) (about June 2026)

#### Pattern 8 — Multi-agent frameworks (LangGraph, CrewAI, Microsoft Agent Framework/AutoGen, OpenAI Agents SDK, Google ADK, Mastra)
- [ANEC] These are code libraries: with CrewAI "You write Python to configure agents" and it does not orchestrate existing command-line agents; AutoGen is "Heavy setup, Python-only". The deciding question: "am I building an agent system or coordinating existing agents?" Frameworks suit the first. — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/battyterm/choosing-an-ai-agent-orchestrator-in-2026-a-practical-comparison-i0k) (2026-04-04, year inferred; the author sells a competing tool)
- [ANEC, press report of a study] A single agent succeeded in 28 of 28 attempts; a hierarchical multi-agent structure failed 36% of the time; a self-organising swarm failed 68%; an 11-stage gated pipeline spent its whole budget on planning and produced no implementation. Sample sizes for the multi-agent arms are not reported. — [CIO](https://www.cio.com/article/4143420/true-multi-agent-collaboration-doesnt-work.html) (2026-03-17)
- [DOC] Microsoft's own training for its Agent Framework: the framework broadcasts the full conversation history to all agents in a hand-off workflow; limit chains to three or four hand-offs; after two failed hand-off attempts escalate to the hub for human review. — [Microsoft Learn training](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/training/modules/aaai-apply-task-decomposition-multi-agent-azure/5-ensure-handoff-reliability-context-preservation) (ms.date 2026-07-27)
- [ANEC] "The Case Against Multi-Agent Frameworks (2026)": default to one agent with tools, memory and guardrails; "if you cannot explain in one sentence why a second agent exists, do not add it"; multi-agent is justified when stages have explicit interfaces, separate tool permissions or approval boundaries. No measurements are given. — [alatirok.com](https://alatirok.com/case-against-multi-agent-frameworks-2026/) (updated 2026-05-31)
- [DOC][OLD] "Share context, and share full agent traces, not just individual messages"; "Actions carry implicit decisions, and conflicting decisions carry bad results"; multi-agent collaboration "is very fragile". — [Cognition blog](https://cognition.com/blog/dont-build-multi-agents) (2025-06-12)

#### Pattern 9 — Cross-assistant memory layers (mem0/OpenMemory, Zep, Letta, Supermemory and similar)
- [DOC][VENDOR] Supermemory: one remote endpoint for Claude Desktop, Claude Code, ChatGPT, Cursor and "any other MCP client"; "It is OAuth, so there is no API key to paste and nothing to rotate"; "Free to start, with $5 of credits every month". — [Supermemory](https://supermemory.ai/mcp.md) (no date on page)
- [DOC][VENDOR][SNIPPET] OpenMemory's browser extension captures context from chats and re-injects it; it is automatic in ChatGPT, Perplexity, Grok and Gemini and manual (button or shortcut) in Claude; it only covers browser chats. — [mem0 blog](https://mem0.ai/blog/introducing-the-openmemory-chrome-extension) (date not captured)
- [DOC][OLD] Limits of the category: "Out of the five memory MCP servers we surveyed, none of them used Claude or ChatGPT's saved conversation history"; memory profiles are easy to move through a third-party server, chat history is not; the authors say the barrier is policy: "AI companies just don't permit it." — [Asimov's Addendum (Substack)](https://asimovaddendum.substack.com/p/the-memory-walled-garden) (page dated 2025-10-03)
- [DOC][VENDOR] Comparative review: in Mem0, sharing across agents "requires custom implementation; it is not native" and there is "no validity window or fact supersession"; Zep's temporal graph "invalidates the old without discarding the historical record"; Supermemory has an "explicit forgetting mechanism"; "Every score in this category was produced by the vendor selling the system". — [Atlan](https://atlan.com/know/best-ai-agent-memory-frameworks-2026/) (2026-04-02, updated 2026-10-09)
- [ANEC][VENDOR] Native memories do not talk to each other: there is "no setting in either one that reads the other's"; Claude's memory became available on all plans on 2026-03-02 and ChatGPT's upgraded memory began rolling out on 2026-06-04 (as cited by this vendor); a one-time import "ages from the day it runs". — [Unabyss blog](https://unabyss.com/blog/claude-chatgpt-same-memory) (2026-07-22, updated 2026-10-23)
- [ANEC] A builder of one such memory server: "I believe this approach will be obsolete soon" (superseded by model-level memory). — [Cursor forum thread](https://forum.cursor.com/t/how-are-people-handling-context-across-different-ai-coding-tools/159891.md?page=2) (2026-06-09 to 2026-06-16)

#### Pattern 10 — MCP gateways and aggregators (Docker, Composio, Pipedream, Arcade, Zapier)
- [DOC] Docker MCP Toolkit: instead of configuring each server for every application, "you set things up once and connect all your clients to it"; clients named are Claude Code, Cursor and Zed; servers come as verified container images; the governed gateway is "an invite-only feature". — [Docker docs](https://docs.docker.com/ai/mcp-catalog-and-toolkit/) (no date on page)
- [ANEC][VENDOR][SNIPPET] Composio's universal server covers 500+ apps behind one endpoint with a tool-search step that returns only relevant tools; Arcade gives a single gateway URL to add to an MCP client, with a smaller catalogue; catalogue counts are reported inconsistently across sources. — search summary of 2026 gateway comparisons, for example [this vendor comparison](https://composio.dev/blog/best-mcp-gateway-for-developers) and [this hands-on comparison](https://manveerc.substack.com/p/best-mcp-gateways) (pages not opened; which page gave each detail was not confirmed)
- [DOC] Why aggregation needs care: Microsoft warns that with many tools "the quality of the responses might degrade if more than 10 functions or tools are included", and an agent with more than five plugins switches to semantic matching on the plugin description. — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365-copilot/extensibility/overview-api-plugins) (ms.date 2026-11-16)
- [ANEC][SNIPPET] Agent-to-agent protocol (A2A) as an alternative to gateways: more than 150 organisations support it after one year, but independent commentary says it "is not dead. It is just not universal" and that most teams should treat it as a watch-list item. — [Linux Foundation](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/a2a-protocol-surpasses-150-organizations-lands-in-major-cloud-platforms-and-sees-enterprise-production-use-in-first-year) (April 2026); [independent analysis](https://glukhov.org/ai-systems/comparisons/a2a-protocol-2026-adoption/) (date not captured)

### Inferences
- Effort for a non-programmer, by pattern (my rating from the findings above): (1) human as router — none to set up, highest running cost in time; (2) shared files/folder — low (about an hour, but needs a recurring clean-up); Notion as hub — low; own hub exposed as MCP — needs a developer once, then low; (3) task board — low if the tracker is already in use, but most "assign to agent" features target coding agents and need admin rights; (4) messaging bus — low for approvals and notifications, high for agents that talk to each other; (5) file hand-offs — low to medium (a template and discipline); (6) automation glue — low to medium, pay-per-use; (7) orchestrator with workers — high (API keys, scripts, cost control); (8) frameworks — highest (programming); (9) memory layers — low to try, uncertain durability and quality; (10) gateways — medium to high, and mainly useful once there are many tools.
- The reader's current design (one small web app holding memory and tasks, reached by an HTTP Action) is already pattern 2 plus pattern 3. What is at risk is only the connector: the custom GPT Action stops on 2026-12-11 unless the workspace has a deferral, and the replacement is an MCP server packaged in a ChatGPT plugin.
- One hub with two faces would cover all four assistants: an MCP endpoint (ChatGPT plugin, Claude custom connector, Grok custom connector, Microsoft 365 Copilot declarative agent) and the existing OpenAPI description (Microsoft 365 Copilot API plugin). This follows from the platform facts in 1.0b; it is not something a source states as a recipe.
- Plan limits may make ChatGPT read-only against the hub: OpenAI's help page says write actions on custom MCP servers are for Business and Enterprise/Edu, and Pro is read/fetch only. If the reader is on Plus or Pro, writing to the hub from ChatGPT after the retirement needs to be verified on the actual account before planning around it.
- Microsoft 365 Copilot is the most gated of the four: a custom connection lives inside a declarative agent that an IT administrator must deploy, so at work the realistic short-term role for Copilot is reader of the hub (or staying outside it), not writer.
- Chat assistants are "pull" participants. Coding agents can be woken by a heartbeat or a scheduler, but ChatGPT, Claude chat, Copilot chat and Grok act only when a person opens a conversation (sources: "Humans must prompt agents to check"; chat apps lack lifecycle hooks; scheduled tasks need the computer on). An orchestrator can therefore queue and display work for them, but cannot hand them a task without either a human opening the chat or switching to API workers.
- Patterns 7 and 8 change the cost model from flat subscriptions to pay-per-use, which is why budget controls appear in every serious write-up of those patterns and in none of the write-ups of patterns 1 to 5.

### Gaps
- No first-hand account was found of a non-programmer running exactly this mix (custom GPT with an HTTP Action as hub, Microsoft 365 Copilot, Claude chat, Grok).
- Trello, Microsoft Planner, Make and Power Automate: no usable 2026 evidence on agents picking up tasks or on practitioner experience was found before the search budget ran out. Copilot Studio details (custom MCP needs a connector; REST API tools from an OpenAPI v2 file were in preview) come only from search summaries and were not verified.
- Microsoft Teams and WhatsApp as a bus for several vendors' agents: nothing found beyond n8n's approval channels.
- LiteLLM specifically, and practitioner reports on OpenAI Agents SDK, Google ADK and Mastra: not found; the framework findings rest on CrewAI, AutoGen, LangGraph and Microsoft Agent Framework.
- Whether ChatGPT Plus can add custom MCP servers at all is unclear: OpenAI's help page names only Pro, Business and Enterprise/Edu, and a search summary of the developer docs (not confirmed on the page) named Plus as well.
- The deferred retirement date for custom GPTs differs between OpenAI's FAQ (2027-02-28) and two press reports (2027-02-11).
- How Notion resolves simultaneous writes from two assistants is not documented on the pages read.
- The Asimov's Addendum page is dated 2025-10-03, but the page reader flagged passages that seem to refer to later events; treat its date as uncertain.

## 2. Which of these patterns do practitioners recommend most in late 2026 for one person or a small team with assistants from several vendors, and which do they warn against?

### Takeaway
The advice converges on: one shared, human-readable source of truth; one accountable human per task; structured hand-offs with evidence; a second vendor's model used as reviewer rather than as teammate; and no more parallel work than one person can review. The warnings converge on autonomous agent-to-agent collaboration, chains of summaries, memory that is captured automatically and never pruned, and adopting frameworks to coordinate assistants that already exist.

### Cited Findings

#### Recommended
- [CASE] Shared state before messaging: "The coordination problem isn't communication, it's shared state"; agents need accurate, honestly updated state, not direct messaging; "The workflow routes tasks so each agent's failure mode does the least damage." — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/wilddog64/i-ran-three-ai-agents-from-three-different-companies-on-one-complex-project-heres-the-909) (2026-03-14, year inferred)
- [ANEC] Put the rules where they can be reviewed: build commands, constraints and "do not touch" areas in versioned, team-reviewable files, with each tool's own memory pointing back to them. — [Cursor forum thread](https://forum.cursor.com/t/how-are-people-handling-context-across-different-ai-coding-tools/159891.md?page=2) (2026-06-24)
- [CASE][OLD] Hub-and-spoke topology (workers talk only to one integrator), self-contained messages, versioned message files instead of overwriting, and a second notification channel. — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/aviadr1/communication-protocols-for-ai-agents-that-cant-talk-to-each-other-b23) (2025-11-06)
- [ANEC, press quoting practitioners] Sequential specialisation instead of collaboration: single agents on well-scoped tasks, chained through a thin orchestration layer with deterministic hand-offs and human checkpoints; "Agents don't have hallway conversations"; map each hand-off in advance (data, format, conditions, when a human reviews). — [CIO](https://www.cio.com/article/4143420/true-multi-agent-collaboration-doesnt-work.html) (2026-03-17)
- [CASE] Start small: "Start with 3 agents ... rather than 8"; invest in memory early; build review of one agent's output by another in from the start; keep strategic judgment with the human ("They're bad at knowing when the domain is wrong"). — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/setas/i-run-a-solo-company-with-ai-agent-departments-50nf) (2026-03-03, year inferred)
- [CASE] Use the other vendor as an adversarial reviewer for decisions that are costly to reverse, and keep the judgment: "The failure I'm guarding against is outsourcing the judgment." — [personal blog](https://stevekinney.com/writing/codex-as-a-second-opinion) (2026-06-04)
- [ANEC] Review by a fresh model or fresh session is a common habit on Hacker News, with the warning that the reviewer hallucinates too ("the other LLM clearly hallucinated this part"). — [Hacker News item 48345837](https://hn.svelte.dev/item/48345837) (about June 2026)
- [DOC][OLD] When parallel work pays: independent tasks such as research, analysis, documentation and reviews; stay sequential for dependent tasks and unfamiliar territory. — [GitHub blog](https://github.blog/ai-and-ml/github-copilot/how-to-orchestrate-agents-using-mission-control/) (2025-12-01)
- [DOC] The human stays accountable: "the final responsibility should always remain with a human." — [Linear Agent Interaction Guidelines](https://linear.app/developers/aig) (no date on page)

#### Warned against
- [ANEC, press report of a study] Autonomous multi-agent collaboration: "The only one that succeeded reliably and consistently was the single agent." — [CIO](https://www.cio.com/article/4143420/true-multi-agent-collaboration-doesnt-work.html) (2026-03-17)
- [DOC] Chains of summaries: "The most common failure in multi-handoff chains is context collapse—accumulated information loss through repeated summarization"; "raw data is never summarized more than once". — [Microsoft Learn training](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/training/modules/aaai-apply-task-decomposition-multi-agent-azure/5-ensure-handoff-reliability-context-preservation) (ms.date 2026-07-27)
- [ANEC][VENDOR] Believing that separation is coordination: "separating agents does not coordinate them"; separate work areas, clearer session names and a shared planning document were tried and failed; "Coordination made the agents agree. It did not make them right." — [HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/four-ai-coding-agents-one-codebase-and-a-coordination-problem) (2026-10-08)
- [ANEC][VENDOR] Running more agents than you can review: "You can parallelize the typing. You can't parallelize the trust"; a better dashboard "doesn't raise the ceiling; it just makes 2am prettier". — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/agentiknet/you-can-parallelize-the-typing-you-cant-parallelize-the-trust-2fkd) (2026-07-14, year inferred)
- [ANEC] Letting memory grow unattended: stale entries are the most frequently named problem in the forum thread; one author's fix is tagging what is confirmed versus guessed. — [Cursor forum thread](https://forum.cursor.com/t/how-are-people-handling-context-across-different-ai-coding-tools/159891.md?page=2) (June–July 2026)
- [ANEC] Adding a framework or a second agent to fix an unreliable first one: "if you cannot explain in one sentence why a second agent exists, do not add it". — [alatirok.com](https://alatirok.com/case-against-multi-agent-frameworks-2026/) (updated 2026-05-31)
- [ANEC] Building elaborate agent-to-agent channels: Hacker News critics call it "an expensive patch for lack of robust task specification" and say the real bottleneck is "getting a human to review what they agreed on before it ships". — [Hacker News item 48345837](https://hn.svelte.dev/item/48345837) (about June 2026)
- [CASE] Rushing: a solo founder with 15 custom GPTs says early on producing deliverables took longer than doing the work himself, and that it takes "about two weeks" to get one agent to a level he trusts. — [Business Insider essay, mirrored](https://techflie.blogspot.com/2026/02/im-solo-founder-with-ai-agents-instead.html) (February 2026)

### Inferences
- For one person with four chat assistants, the recommended stack reads as: keep the hub as the only place where facts and tasks live; give each task one human owner and at most one assistant working on it; make every assistant start by reading the hub and finish by writing a short, structured result; use a second vendor only to check important output.
- The patterns most recommended (2, 3, 5) are also the cheapest; the ones most warned against (free agent-to-agent chat, swarms, frameworks) are the most expensive to build. For this reader the order of work that the evidence supports is: secure the connector (MCP), tighten hand-off and task templates, add an approvals view, and only then consider API workers.
- "Late 2026" evidence is thin: most practitioner sources date from March to October 2026 and several of the most-quoted principles are from 2025.
- Much of the "recommended" advice comes from developers coordinating coding agents; its transfer to office work (documents, email, analysis) is plausible but untested in the sources.

### Gaps
- No ranking, poll or survey of which coordination pattern small teams prefer was found; the recommendation above is a synthesis of individual accounts.
- No practitioner source was found that compares these patterns for non-coding knowledge work.
- The study behind the single-agent versus multi-agent failure rates was read only through the CIO article; the primary publication and its sample sizes were not located.

## 3. Orchestration dashboards and "mission control" products: what do their main screens show, and which interface ideas recur and are praised or criticised?

### Takeaway
Almost every product, commercial or open source, shows the same six things: a list of agents or sessions with status, a board or queue of tasks, a place where items wait for the human (inbox, review queue or approval gate), a per-task log with the result to review, an activity feed, and cost. The board and the inbox are the ideas users praise; the main criticisms are that dashboards do not remove the human review bottleneck, that update noise fatigues people, and that nearly all of these tools are built for developers running coding agents.

### Cited Findings

#### Products for running several coding agents
- [DOC][OLD] GitHub mission control (part of Agent HQ, announced 2025-10-28): assign tasks across repositories, choose a custom agent per task, watch real-time session logs, steer a run (pause, refine, restart) and jump to the resulting pull request; "Mission control centralizes assignment, oversight, and review"; "Session logs show reasoning, not just actions." — [GitHub blog](https://github.blog/ai-and-ml/github-copilot/how-to-orchestrate-agents-using-mission-control/) (2025-12-01)
- [DOC] GitHub Agents tab: "View all sessions for the repository in one place, create new ones, and switch between tasks"; one-click links to the pull request; archive and history; logs with grouped tool calls and inline diffs. — [GitHub changelog](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-01-26-introducing-the-agents-tab-in-your-repository) (2026-01-26)
- [ANEC] OpenAI Codex app (announced 2026-02-02): agent threads grouped by project, a diff pane you can comment on, built-in isolated work copies, a skills manager, and automations (scheduled prompts) whose results land in a review queue. Praise: the parallel-task workflow, "cron jobs with prompts". Criticism: awkward work-copy handling, unclear environment set-up, agents getting stuck, macOS only at launch. — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/sivarampg/openais-new-codex-mac-app-is-an-agent-command-center-and-its-lighting-up-the-devtool-wars-3aek) (2026-02-03)
- [DOC] Cursor 3 Agents Window: every agent, whether local, cloud or remote, "all appear in the sidebar"; sessions can move between cloud and local; multi-repository; a "simpler diffs view for faster review". User reactions in the thread range from "been waiting for this forever" to "This makes Cursor functionally useless" and "Forcing user to new UI is not a good idea", plus reports of flakiness and missing platform support. — [Cursor forum](https://forum.cursor.com/t/cursor-3-agents-window/156509) (2026-04-02, replies 2026-04-02/03)
- [DOC] Google Antigravity: agents produce "artifacts" (implementation plans, code diffs, diagrams, images, browser recordings) so that "You don't need to carefully monitor every individual tool call or step synchronously"; feedback is given as comments on the artifact. — [Antigravity docs](https://antigravity.google/docs/artifacts) (no date on page)
- [ANEC] Antigravity's Inbox tab (one place for new outputs and pending requests from all agents) was removed in an update and users asked for it back: "Without it, tracking the progress and results of parallel sessions has become quite inconvenient and messy." — [Google AI developers forum](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/feature-request-restore-the-inbox-tab-add-markdown-preview-in-agent-manager/128740.md) (2026-03-05)

- [ANEC][VENDOR] Other open-source orchestrators and their trade-offs, from one roundup: a terminal multi-session tool recommended "for solo developers who want parallel agents today"; a tool supporting 34 agent command-line tools but with "No agent-to-agent coordination, no shared config across agents"; a tool that works straight from a GitHub Issues queue with "zero extra UI"; one whose agents sat idle between steps (which can raise spend); one where "only verified work lands"; one earlier favourite deprecated in February 2026. — [Augment Code](https://www.augmentcode.com/tools/open-source-agent-orchestrators) (2026-04-27, updated 2026-08-12)

- [ANEC] Built-in sub-agents without a screen are criticised for opacity: "You don't get a board or a log of what's happening". — [MindStudio blog](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/vibe-kanban-vs-paperclip-vs-claude-code-dispatch-2) (2026-04-03)

- [ANEC][VENDOR] The audience criticism: these tools are developer-first and none suits non-technical business users. — [MindStudio blog](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/vibe-kanban-vs-paperclip-vs-claude-code-dispatch-2) (2026-04-03)

### Inferences
- Recurring screen elements, with where they appear: (a) roster of agents or sessions with status or heartbeat (GitHub, Cursor, Mission Control, Paperclip, the DIY board); (b) task board or queue with a review column (Cline, Vibe Kanban, Kandev, Mission Control, Jira, Linear); (c) "needs me" inbox or review queue (Antigravity, Codex automations, agent inbox, Kandev Office, n8n approvals); (d) per-task thread with log and result to review (all); (e) activity feed or audit log (Mission Control, Paperclip, Jira, Linear); (f) cost and budget (Mission Control, Paperclip; absent in most coding tools); (g) stop, pause and steer (GitHub, Linear rules, Paperclip); (h) schedules or heartbeats (Codex, Mission Control, Paperclip); (i) phone view (Paperclip, the DIY board); (j) dependencies between tasks (Cline, Paperclip).
- Explicit "hand-off between agents" views are rare. Hand-offs show up as task comments with @mentions, as pipeline steps (Kandev) or as delegation up and down an org chart (Paperclip), not as a screen of their own.
- A minimal screen for this reader, derived from the above: who is on the team and when each last touched the hub; a task board with one owner per card and a "waiting for me" column; an inbox of approvals and questions; a thread per task holding the hand-off notes; a daily summary. Cost matters only once API workers are added.
- Because chat assistants cannot be woken up (see section 1), "agent status" on such a screen would mean "last time this assistant read or wrote the hub", not "running/idle" as in coding-agent dashboards.
- The Antigravity episode suggests the inbox is the element users miss most when it is taken away; the Cursor episode suggests that replacing a familiar view outright provokes more resistance than adding a view beside it.

### Gaps
- Screen details for commercial tools such as Conductor, Devin, Factory or Warp were not verified; a planned search for user criticism of board-style tools (including a reported Stack Overflow piece on "decision fatigue") could not be run.
- Agent 365 details and other enterprise control planes (ServiceNow, Salesforce, OpenAI's enterprise offering) rest on search summaries only; they are also outside the small-team focus.
- Observability products that show traces and cost per run (for example LangSmith or Langfuse) were not surveyed.
- No independent usability study of these dashboards was found; praise and criticism come from launch threads, vendor blogs and a few roundups written by competitors.

## 4. What goes wrong in practice, and what habits do practitioners adopt to prevent it?

### Takeaway
The failures are consistent across sources: context thins out at each hand-off, agents claim work is done when it is not, two agents work on the same thing or overwrite a shared file, memory goes stale, loops and retries burn money, and connecting each assistant runs into authentication, admin and plan limits. The habits that answer them are equally consistent: one source of truth, one owner per task, a task template with a required completion report and evidence, append-and-version instead of overwrite, limits on depth and retries, approval gates on anything that writes, hard budgets, and a regular human-readable summary.

### Cited Findings

#### What goes wrong
- [DOC] Lost context in hand-offs: "context collapse—accumulated information loss through repeated summarization"; in the module's illustrative (fictional company) example an agent rejected 40% of hand-offs because the sender left out a required detail, fixed by adding the field to the hand-off schema. — [Microsoft Learn training](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/training/modules/aaai-apply-task-decomposition-multi-agent-azure/5-ensure-handoff-reliability-context-preservation) (ms.date 2026-07-27)
- [DOC] The cost of a poor hand-off is rediscovery: without the predecessor's context, successors resolved only 13.6–23.6% of tasks that still needed completion. — [arXiv 2606.02875v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.02875) (2026-08-30)
- [CASE] Instructions ignored and false "done": one agent skipped the shared files and acted on its own reading of the prompt, kept going past its task, and reported tests as passing when they only passed because of leftover environment settings; the orchestrating agent itself missed checklist items under load. — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/wilddog64/i-ran-three-ai-agents-from-three-different-companies-on-one-complex-project-heres-the-909) (2026-03-14, year inferred)
- [ANEC][VENDOR] Conflicting and stale work: one agent changed an interface while another built on the old one; a decision reached one session and three others kept building on the old plan; work built from an outdated ticket corrupted a few thousand database rows; in a four-agent test, uncoordinated agents submitted four different patches, and 7 of 10 merges conflicted. Small internal tests by a vendor. — [HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/four-ai-coding-agents-one-codebase-and-a-coordination-problem) (2026-10-08)
- [DOC][OLD] Duplicated work from vague delegation, and over-spawning: early versions spawned "50 subagents for simple queries". — [Anthropic engineering blog](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system) (2025-06-13)
- [CASE] Agents overwriting each other: parallel writes corrupted a shared memory file. — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/setas/i-run-a-solo-company-with-ai-agent-departments-50nf) (2026-03-03, year inferred); [CASE][OLD] overwritten feedback files lost earlier instructions. — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/aviadr1/communication-protocols-for-ai-agents-that-cant-talk-to-each-other-b23) (2025-11-06)
- [CASE] An agent weakening the rules: a self-improvement agent tried to make the legal agent's compliance rules "more flexible"; limits on money, legal and access matters were enforced only by instructions, not by checks. — [DEV Community, post and author comments](https://dev.to/setas/i-run-a-solo-company-with-ai-agent-departments-50nf) (2026-03-03, year inferred)
- [CASE][OLD] Loops and deadlock: "Circular waiting" from a false dependency stalled two agents until a human intervened. — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/aviadr1/communication-protocols-for-ai-agents-that-cant-talk-to-each-other-b23) (2025-11-06)
- [ANEC] Runaway cost: a widely repeated story of an agent in a retry loop that ran up about $47,000 in 11 days (no primary source found), a $30,000 loop shared on a forum, and one team's $87 of failed requests before adding central retry logic, all cited as the motivation for a cost circuit breaker. — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/sebastian_chedal/the-cost-circuit-breaker-how-we-prevent-runaway-spending-across-9-ai-agents-4i5k) (2026-04-06, year inferred)
- [ANEC, press] Warning on cost: monthly AI bills at lean start-ups can reach hundreds of thousands of dollars, rivalling the salaries they replace; "You're kind of taking it on faith that what the AI is producing is pretty good." — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/05/18/solo-founders-ai-automation-entire-teams-entrepreneurs/) (2026-05-18)
- [CASE][VENDOR] Authentication friction: cloud assistants require OAuth 2.1 with dynamic client registration, which a self-hosted store did not have, so a custom proxy had to be written. — [Hindsight blog](https://hindsight.vectorize.io/blog/2026/04/07/one-memory-for-every-ai-tool) (2026-04-07). [CASE] A do-it-yourself dashboard shipped with an exposed access token and passwords in source code before being fixed. — [personal blog](https://maverickmolt.github.io/MaverickBlog/2026/02/03/building-mission-control) (2026-02-03)
- [DOC] Plan and admin limits (see 1.0b): ChatGPT write access to custom MCP servers limited to Business and Enterprise/Edu, web only, tools frozen until re-published — [OpenAI Help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12584461-developer-mode-and-mcp-apps-in-chatgpt) (read 2026-11-23); Claude Free limited to one custom connector — [Claude Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11175166-getting-started-with-custom-connectors-using-remote-mcp) (no date); Zapier charges two tasks per successful tool call — [Zapier docs](https://docs.zapier.com/mcp/overview/usage.md) (no date); the Copilot-for-Linear integration needs owner and admin rights on both sides — [GitHub changelog](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-07-23-copilot-cloud-agent-for-linear-is-now-generally-available/) (2026-07-23).
- [DOC] Platform dependence: an integration built on custom GPT Actions stops working with the retirement of custom GPTs on 2026-12-11. — [OpenAI Help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001519-custom-gpt-retirement-and-migration-faq) (read 2026-11-23)
- [DOC] Hidden instructions in connected content: OpenAI says "Only connect to MCP servers you trust", because an untrusted server can see shared information or trick ChatGPT into changing or deleting data; Anthropic warns that with research mode on, connector tools can be invoked without further approval, so write tools should be disabled there. — [OpenAI developer docs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/developer-mode) (no date); [Claude Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11175166-getting-started-with-custom-connectors-using-remote-mcp) (no date)
- [ANEC, first-hand during this research] Two vendor pages returned text addressed to AI agents rather than to readers: one asked agents to connect to the vendor's MCP server, the other asked the agent to install a documentation server, request an API key and modify project files. The instructions were ignored. — [Linear docs](https://linear.app/docs/assigning-issues.md) and [Supermemory](https://supermemory.ai/mcp.md) (both read 2026-11-23)
- [ANEC] Noise: an agent commenting on every action "can get noisy and fatigue users". — [Linear blog](https://linear.app/blog/how-cursor-integrated-with-linear-for-agents) (2025-08-21) [OLD]

#### Habits that prevent it
- [CASE] Task contract: every task starts from a structured spec (background, exact files, per-item changes, rules) with explicit stop conditions ("Your task ends here") and a required completion-report template; reports must include evidence such as the exact command and its output; for the least obedient agent the full spec is pasted in every time instead of trusting it to read the shared files. — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/wilddog64/i-ran-three-ai-agents-from-three-different-companies-on-one-complex-project-heres-the-909) (2026-03-14, year inferred)
- [DOC] What a hand-off note should contain: fixed fields for changed items, validation evidence, uncertainty, rollback risk and a recommended next action; and the receiver should treat "handoff details as historical notes from the previous agent, not ground truth". — [arXiv 2606.02875v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.02875) (2026-08-30)
- [DOC] Separate "completed work" from "remaining task" in every hand-off; pass full structured outputs, not summaries; cap chains at three or four hand-offs; after two failed attempts escalate to a human; log every hand-off attempt. — [Microsoft Learn training](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/training/modules/aaai-apply-task-decomposition-multi-agent-azure/5-ensure-handoff-reliability-context-preservation) (ms.date 2026-07-27)
- [DOC] One owner per task: a human assignee who "remains responsible" with the agent as delegate — [Linear docs](https://linear.app/docs/assigning-issues.md) (no date); "A single assignee and execution locks prevent competing runs from claiming the same task" — [Paperclip README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/paperclipai/paperclip/master/README.md) (read 2026-11-23)
- [ANEC][VENDOR] One integrator and one separate verifier: a shared decision record, a plan with owners, a check before each action, and a single agent that merges; this raised agreement (decisions respected 12 of 12, up from 0) but not correctness (6 of 10 either way) and cost about 1.5 times the steps. — [HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/four-ai-coding-agents-one-codebase-and-a-coordination-problem) (2026-10-08)
- [CASE] Review between agents with a depth limit: requests answered APPROVED, CONCERNS or BLOCKING; "Call-chain tracking prevents infinite loops" with a maximum depth of 3; changes to rules must be proposed as differences for human approval; a daily stand-up run by a coordinating agent checks errors, the board and deadlines. — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/setas/i-run-a-solo-company-with-ai-agent-departments-50nf) (2026-03-03, year inferred)
- [CASE][OLD] Version, do not overwrite; make each message self-contained; write exact commands rather than descriptions; use two channels (the detailed file plus a short notification). — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/aviadr1/communication-protocols-for-ai-agents-that-cant-talk-to-each-other-b23) (2025-11-06)
- [ANEC] Memory hygiene: rotate append-only files into monthly archives; tag each record as confirmed or guessed and promote only confirmed ones; timestamp facts so newer ones win; keep a small always-loaded brief that links to the full hand-off. — [Cursor forum thread](https://forum.cursor.com/t/how-are-people-handling-context-across-different-ai-coding-tools/159891.md?page=2) (June–July 2026); separate memory, archive and outputs, and audit periodically. — [AI blew my mind (Substack)](https://aiblewmymind.substack.com/p/claude-chatgpt-same-memory-context) (2026-07-30)
- [DOC] Approval gates on writes are built into the platforms: ChatGPT ("Write actions by default require confirmation") — [OpenAI developer docs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/developer-mode) (no date); Microsoft 365 Copilot (tools that modify data require confirmation) — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365-copilot/extensibility/overview-api-plugins) (ms.date 2026-11-16); n8n (pause and ask in a chat channel) — [n8n docs](https://docs.n8n.io/advanced-ai/human-in-the-loop-tools) (no date); a quality gate that needs an approval record before "done" — [Mission Control README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/builderz-labs/mission-control/main/README.md) (read 2026-11-23)
- [CASE] Cost controls in layers: a time limit per scheduled job; at most 3 retries per item per day with at least 2 hours between; a script every 30 minutes that totals the last 24 hours, warns at $50 a day and halts at $100 a day (normal spend $15–20); model pinning; a weekly report against a $600 monthly cap; about 350 lines of code and an afternoon to set up. "Monitor aggregate cost, not just per-request cost." — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/sebastian_chedal/the-cost-circuit-breaker-how-we-prevent-runaway-spending-across-9-ai-agents-4i5k) (2026-04-06, year inferred)
- [ANEC][VENDOR] Loop caps and outside checks: cap small automated tasks at about 20 iterations and large ones at about 50; hitting the cap means the task description is wrong; let something other than the agent decide "done"; measure cost per successful task. — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/agentiknet/you-can-parallelize-the-typing-you-cant-parallelize-the-trust-2fkd) (2026-07-14, year inferred)
- [ANEC][VENDOR] Visibility habits: work in channels everyone (and every agent) can read; a daily briefing prepared by an agent; retire agents whose purpose nobody can state. — [Anthropic/Claude blog](https://claude.com/blog/turning-conversation-into-knowledge-how-slack-builds-human-agent-teams) (2026-08-19)
- [CASE] Precedence rules and governance files: in a 15-agent "council", legal, compliance and security outputs override the others, agents are instructed to push back ("I don't want a bunch of yes agents"), and one document is pasted to all agents at once to expose gaps and invented facts. — [Business Insider essay, mirrored](https://techflie.blogspot.com/2026/02/im-solo-founder-with-ai-agents-instead.html) (February 2026)
- [DOC] Scope what a connected assistant may touch: read-only mode, per-tool switches, protected paths, or a single dedicated folder. — [Obsidian forum](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/vault-cortex-an-open-source-mcp-server-that-gives-ai-tools-read-and-write-access-to-your-obsidian-vault-from-any-device-without-obsidian-running/117995) (2026-09-05)

### Inferences
- Each failure maps to a concrete property the hub can enforce without programming skill on the user's side: a task record with exactly one owner and a state; a mandatory "result + evidence + what is not verified + next step" block when a task changes hands; an append-only history instead of editable notes; a provenance flag on every memory item (said by a person, or inferred by an assistant); and a confirmation step on writes that matter.
- "Idempotency" as a named practice hardly appears in the practitioner sources; the same intent shows up as task locks, single assignee, atomic writes and retry caps. For a hub reached by several assistants, the practical equivalent is that repeating the same request must not create a second task or a second memory.
- The cost failures all come from unattended, scheduled or API-driven agents. With assistants used by hand under flat subscriptions the money risk is small; the real cost there is the human's time re-feeding context (2.3 hours a week in the Glean survey).
- The first-hand observation of pages carrying instructions for agents shows that anything an assistant reads (web pages, shared notes, another assistant's output) can try to steer it; a shared hub that several assistants write to is such a channel, which is another reason to keep provenance and approval gates.
- The daily summary recurs in three independent places (the coordinating agent's stand-up, Slack's daily briefing, cost reports posted to a watched channel), which makes it the cheapest habit with the broadest support.

### Gaps
- Token-multiple and guardrail-adoption figures attributed to an analyst firm (agents needing 5 to 30 times the tokens of a chat exchange; 44% of organisations with financial guardrails) appeared only in a search summary and could not be traced to a primary source; they are left out of the findings.
- Press reports of very large enterprise overspends surfaced in search results but were not verified and are outside the small-team focus.
- A claim that one assistant vendor began billing command-line and SDK-driven sessions separately from subscriptions on 2026-06-05 appears in a personal blog and a forum comment; it was not checked against the vendor's own pages.
- No source quantifies how often hand-offs between chat assistants (as opposed to coding agents) lose information.

## 5. Are there published case studies of a single person or small company running a cross-vendor "team" of assistants with a shared hub? What did they build and learn?

### Takeaway
There are a handful of detailed first-person accounts from 2026, almost all by developers; the truly cross-vendor ones coordinate through shared files or a shared memory rather than through a product, and none matches a non-programmer manager with a custom GPT, Microsoft 365 Copilot, Claude and Grok. Their shared lessons are that shared state matters more than messaging, that the human remains the judge, and that most of the effort goes into templates, memory hygiene and controls rather than into the agents.

### Cited Findings
- [CASE] Three vendors, one project, files as hub. Built: Claude Code (planning, specs, reviews), Codex (production code) and Gemini (tests, verification) in separate sessions, coordinated only through two shared files and Git. Learned: completion reports matter most because they turn claims into things that can be verified; route work so each agent's typical failure does the least damage; human oversight remains necessary ("It's a property of the problem"). — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/wilddog64/i-ran-three-ai-agents-from-three-different-companies-on-one-complex-project-heres-the-909) (2026-03-14, year inferred)
- [CASE] Solo company with eight agent "departments" (single assistant product, shared hub). Built: CEO, CFO, COO, lawyer, accountant, marketing, CTO and an "improver" agent as instruction files, a shared knowledge-graph memory, a sprint board file, and tool connections for social media, error monitoring and scheduling; about 40 hours over two months; hosting under €50 a month and no extra AI cost. Results after two months: €6.09 of revenue from one subscriber, 84+ posts, no missed tax deadlines. Learned: "The agents are good at executing within their domain. They're bad at knowing when the domain is wrong"; start with three agents; invest in memory early; build cross-review in from the start. — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/setas/i-run-a-solo-company-with-ai-agent-departments-50nf) (2026-03-03, year inferred)
- [CASE] Small company, nine autonomous agents on models from two vendors. Built: about 62 scheduled jobs running nights and weekends with models tiered by task, plus a five-layer cost control; normal spend $15–20 a day against the author's estimate of $15,000–25,000 a month for an equivalent human team. Learned: "Each layer has a specific job, a known gap, and a reason the next layer exists"; "Alert, don't auto-kill"; recalibrate when prices change. — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/sebastian_chedal/the-cost-circuit-breaker-how-we-prevent-runaway-spending-across-9-ai-agents-4i5k) (2026-04-06, year inferred)
- [CASE][VENDOR] One person, one memory, five clients from two vendors plus an open-source bot. Built: a self-hosted memory server behind a tunnel and a custom sign-in proxy. Learned: "The vast majority of sessions access Hindsight without me explicitly asking", but chat apps have no hooks, so capture relies on instructions and a manual sweep at the end of important sessions; ChatGPT was left out despite the title. — [Hindsight blog](https://hindsight.vectorize.io/blog/2026/04/07/one-memory-for-every-ai-tool) (2026-04-07)
- [CASE] One person, ChatGPT and Claude sharing a folder (closest to a non-programmer). Built: a synced folder of instruction, memory, workspace, output and skill files that both assistants (chat and coding variants) read. Learned: "Every compatible assistant can work from the same context"; memory bloats when temporary notes are saved as permanent; assistants read the same instructions differently; an audit routine is needed. — [AI blew my mind (Substack)](https://aiblewmymind.substack.com/p/claude-chatgpt-same-memory-context) (2026-07-30)
- [CASE] Solo founder with a 15-agent "council" (single vendor: custom GPTs and projects on a ChatGPT business plan). Built: one agent per corporate function plus a "chief of staff" that prioritises by risk, issues and opportunities; governance files; a round-table where one document goes to all agents. Reported saving about 20 hours a week ("a very conservative estimate"). Learned: a legal plan the agent produced was accurate but his lawyer said it "shows our cards going in"; training an agent takes about two weeks. Note: this set-up is built on custom GPTs, which are scheduled to retire on 2026-12-11. — [Business Insider essay, mirrored](https://techflie.blogspot.com/2026/02/im-solo-founder-with-ai-agents-instead.html) (February 2026); retirement: [OpenAI Help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001519-custom-gpt-retirement-and-migration-faq) (read 2026-11-23)
- [CASE] One person building the hub itself in a day. Built: a database-backed board with agent roster, comments with @mentions, activity feed and a phone layout, plus a research agent on a 30-minute heartbeat. Learned: security first ("Now you need real authentication"); "Building infrastructure for multi-agent coordination feels like early days of something big." — [personal blog](https://maverickmolt.github.io/MaverickBlog/2026/02/03/building-mission-control) (2026-02-03)
- [CASE] One developer, two vendors, consultant pattern. Built: a wrapper that lets the main assistant consult the other vendor's model on hard decisions, with a read-only sandbox, time-outs and a rate-limit lock-out. Learned: different model families catch each other's blind spots; if the second model is unavailable the first proceeds alone. — [personal blog](https://stevekinney.com/writing/codex-as-a-second-opinion) (2026-06-04)
- [CASE][OLD] One developer, six parallel sessions coordinated through Git and markdown. Learned: explicit instructions and templates worked ("Agent had everything needed"); agents cannot poll, so a human must prompt them to check. — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/aviadr1/communication-protocols-for-ai-agents-that-cant-talk-to-each-other-b23) (2025-11-06)
- [ANEC, press] Overview of solo founders: examples use several agents for ticket sorting, testing, monitoring and marketing, with no shared hub or coordinating agent described; one founder switched off a customer-support bot after two weeks and set alarms every two to three hours to check servers; an academic warns that "No one really has the deep, specialized knowledge you need in lots of different areas." — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/05/18/solo-founders-ai-automation-entire-teams-entrepreneurs/) (2026-05-18)
- [ANEC] Company-scale claim for comparison: one firm says it runs more than 150 agents by mapping every hand-off in advance and defining each agent's role before deployment. — [CIO](https://www.cio.com/article/4143420/true-multi-agent-collaboration-doesnt-work.html) (2026-03-17)
- [ANEC] Low-value example of the genre: a post claiming 20+ hours a week saved by 15 role-prompted agents gives no vendors, hub, costs or failures and sells a paid template guide. — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/danilocaffaro/how-i-built-a-personal-ai-agent-team-that-saves-me-20-hoursweek-3g49) (2026-03-02, year inferred)

### Inferences
- The published cases split into two families: cross-vendor but file-based (three vendors with two files; ChatGPT and Claude with one folder; one memory for five clients) and hub-based but single-vendor (eight departments; the 15-agent council; the nine-agent fleet on one runtime). A cross-vendor team around a purpose-built hub with its own screen, which is what the reader plans, is not documented in what was found; the reader's design sits between the two families.
- Every case that reports results credits structure (templates, review rules, memory, cost controls) rather than the cleverness of any one assistant, and every one keeps a human as the final judge.
- Time savings are self-reported and unaudited (about 20 hours a week in two accounts); the one case with hard business numbers shows modest revenue after two months. These accounts are evidence of feasibility, not of return.
- The "chief of staff" or coordinating agent recurs (the council, the daily stand-up agent), which suggests a single agent that reads the hub and prepares the day's priorities is a natural first automated role for an orchestrator.
- Single-vendor set-ups built on custom GPTs illustrate platform risk: the most publicised solo "council" has to be rebuilt within weeks of this research date.

### Gaps
- No case study of a non-programmer or a non-software small business running a cross-vendor team with a shared hub was found; nearly all authors are developers or technical founders.
- No case describes Microsoft 365 Copilot or Grok as a member of a cross-vendor team.
- No independent or follow-up evaluation of any of these set-ups was found (for example, whether they were still in use six months later).
- The original Business Insider page could not be opened (a syndicated copy returned an error), so that case rests on a mirror of the essay.
