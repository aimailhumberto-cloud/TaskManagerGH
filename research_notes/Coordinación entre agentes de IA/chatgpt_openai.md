# ChatGPT / OpenAI: how a ChatGPT-based agent connects to external systems and to other vendors' agents (state as of 2026-11-23)

Reading notes for the writer:
- All pages were read on 2026-11-23. OpenAI Help Center pages show only relative dates ("Updated: 5 hours ago"); the approximate calendar date is given in each link label.
- "(snippet only)" means the fact comes from a search-result summary and the page itself was not opened; treat as lower confidence.
- Status labels used: GA = generally available; beta/preview as stated by the source; "announced" = no availability stated.
- Headline for the reader's case: **custom GPTs are being retired (standard date December 11, 2026) and custom Actions do not migrate.** Everything below is organised around that fact.

## 1. Custom GPTs today: current product or being replaced? What Actions can and cannot do; MCP; outside triggers; API; plans

### Takeaway
Custom GPTs are an end-of-life product: OpenAI announced on September 11, 2026 that they will be retired across all ChatGPT plans and replaced by **plugins** (skills + connected apps + optional MCP server); standard retirement is **December 11, 2026**, new GPTs can no longer be created on personal plans, and **custom Actions are not carried over** by the migration tool. A GPT has never been callable by API, cannot run on a schedule or event, has no memory, and its Actions are limited to 45-second, 100,000-character, text-only HTTP calls.

### Cited Findings

**Retirement and replacement**
- "We're planning to retire custom GPTs and help creators move their workflows to plugins." Plugins "bring reusable instructions, reference files, and connected apps together." — [OpenAI Help Center, "GPTs in ChatGPT", updated ~2026-11-23](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8554407-gpts-in-chatgpt)
- First announced in release notes on September 11, 2026: "We're planning to retire custom GPTs across ChatGPT plans and provide a migration path to plugins." — [ChatGPT release notes, entry 2026-09-11](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/6825453-chatgpt-release-notes); same text in [ChatGPT Business release notes, entry 2026-09-11](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11391654-chatgpt-business-release-notes)
- Dates: "Standard retirement is scheduled for December 11, 2026." "FedRAMP workspaces and workspaces with a qualifying, approved deferral have a retirement date of February 28, 2027." "For affected Enterprise workspaces, new GPT creation ended on October 26, 2026." — [Help Center, "GPTs in ChatGPT", updated ~2026-11-23](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8554407-gpts-in-chatgpt)
- Full timeline in the FAQ: Sep 11, 2026 admin notice (Enterprise); Sep 22 target for migration experience and user banner ("a target, not a guarantee"); Oct 1 banner for workspaces with delayed banners; Oct 26 new GPT creation ends for affected Enterprise workspaces (except FedRAMP); Dec 11, 2026 standard retirement; Feb 28, 2027 for FedRAMP / approved deferral ("does not apply to every workspace"). — [Help Center, "Custom GPT retirement and migration FAQ", updated ~2026-11-22](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001519-custom-gpt-retirement-and-migration-faq)
- Personal plans: "New GPT creation and publishing are not available on personal ChatGPT accounts, including Free, Go, Plus, and Pro." For Business/Enterprise/Edu, creation depends on "product availability, workspace settings, and permissions." "The transition includes all ChatGPT plans, but creation restrictions and migration access depend on the account or workspace." — [Help Center, "GPTs in ChatGPT", updated ~2026-11-23](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8554407-gpts-in-chatgpt)
- Until retirement: "Existing GPTs remain available to use." "Existing unmigrated GPTs remain usable and editable until their applicable retirement date." "After migration, the original GPT becomes read-only." "Editing an existing GPT continues to require an eligible subscription." — [Help Center, "GPTs in ChatGPT", updated ~2026-11-23](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8554407-gpts-in-chatgpt)
- At retirement: "Custom GPTs and their GPT pages become inaccessible"; "Your existing conversations with custom GPTs will remain accessible after retirement." — [Help Center, retirement FAQ, updated ~2026-11-22](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001519-custom-gpt-retirement-and-migration-faq)
- What the migration tool moves: instructions become a skill inside the new plugin; knowledge files are copied to the plugin's reference files; connected apps are added as apps. What it does not move: "GPT custom actions do not transfer through the migration workflow"; the selected model does not carry over; public sharing settings do not carry over and the plugin starts private. Rebuilding an action "may require a custom MCP server and technical setup"; "Access to custom integrations depends on the options available to your account or workspace." — [Help Center, retirement FAQ, updated ~2026-11-22](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001519-custom-gpt-retirement-and-migration-faq)
- "Custom actions do not transfer automatically when a GPT is migrated to a plugin." Set up and test a replacement integration before relying on the plugin. — [Help Center, "GPT Actions", updated ~2026-11-23](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9442513-gpt-actions)
- Who can migrate: on individual accounts the GPT's creator (the GPT must be published; publishing does not require public sharing); in Enterprise the creator or a workspace admin. — [Help Center, retirement FAQ, updated ~2026-11-22](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001519-custom-gpt-retirement-and-migration-faq)
- Earlier signal: in April 2026 OpenAI called workspace agents "an evolution of GPTs", said GPTs "will remain available while teams test workspace agents" and "Soon, we'll make it easy to convert GPTs into workspace agents." — [OpenAI, "Introducing workspace agents in ChatGPT", 2026-04-22](https://openai.com/index/introducing-workspace-agents-in-chatgpt/). The path that actually shipped in September 2026 is GPT-to-plugin (sources above).
- Date conflict to note: one search snippet of the Actions help page showed the deferral date as February 11, 2027, and other-language versions of the FAQ showed an earlier draft ("Sep 25, 2026 (planned): Creation of new custom GPTs ends"). The English pages opened on 2026-11-23 say February 28, 2027 and October 26, 2026. — [Help Center, retirement FAQ](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001519-custom-gpt-retirement-and-migration-faq)

**What GPT Actions can do (until retirement)**
- Authentication: None, API key, or OAuth. API key "can be configured as: Basic, Bearer, Custom header". OAuth "Uses user sign-in for account-based access" and needs client ID, client secret, authorization URL, token URL, scope. — [Help Center, "GPT Actions", updated ~2026-11-23](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9442513-gpt-actions); [OpenAI developer docs, "GPT Action authentication", undated (snippet only)](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/actions/authentication)
- "you can mix a single authentication type (OAuth or API key) along with endpoints that do not require authentication." — [OpenAI developer docs, "Production notes on GPT Actions", undated, read 2026-11-23](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/actions/production)
- Limits: "45 seconds round trip for API calls" / "Requests timeout after 45 seconds"; "Request and response payloads must be less than 100,000 characters each"; "Requests and responses can only contain text (no images or video)"; "300 characters max for each API endpoint description/summary field"; "700 characters max for each API parameter description field"; "Custom headers are not supported"; "All traffic to your action must use TLS 1.2 or later on port 443 with a valid public certificate." — [OpenAI developer docs, "Production notes on GPT Actions", undated, read 2026-11-23](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/actions/production)
- Throttling: "ChatGPT will respect 429 response codes and dynamically back off from sending requests to your action"; it also backs off after a number of 429/500 responses in a short period (threshold not stated). — [Production notes on GPT Actions](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/actions/production)
- User confirmation: if `x-openai-isConsequential` is true the operation "must always prompt the user for confirmation before running"; "If the field isn't present, ChatGPT defaults all GET operations to `false` and all other operations to `true`". — [Production notes on GPT Actions](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/actions/production). "Users may be asked to approve actions before they run." — [Help Center, "GPT Actions", updated ~2026-11-23](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9442513-gpt-actions)
- Privacy: "ChatGPT may send parts of their conversation to the action's endpoint." — [Production notes on GPT Actions](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/actions/production)
- Other restrictions: "Actions are not available for Pro mode."; in Enterprise/Edu an admin domain allowlist can block all actions ("No domains are allowed by your workspace's settings."); "A GPT can use either apps or actions, but not both at the same time." — [Help Center, "GPT Actions", updated ~2026-11-23](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9442513-gpt-actions)

**What a GPT cannot do**
- No memory: "GPTs do not use saved memory, custom instructions, or previous conversations." — [Help Center, "GPTs in ChatGPT", updated ~2026-11-23](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8554407-gpts-in-chatgpt)
- No embedding/API: GPTs "are not a way to embed ChatGPT in an external website or application"; developers are directed to the API, which is separate. A person must be signed in to start a conversation ("GPTs are available to all ChatGPT users, but users must be signed in to start a conversation"). On web a person can "type @ in a conversation to bring in a GPT". — [Help Center, "GPTs in ChatGPT", updated ~2026-11-23](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8554407-gpts-in-chatgpt)
- No schedule or event start: "Scheduled tasks do not support voice chats, GPTs, or locked chats." — [Help Center, "Scheduled tasks in ChatGPT", updated ~2026-10-31](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10291617)
- Not usable in projects: "Chats created with a GPT can't be moved into a project." — [Help Center, "Projects in ChatGPT" (snippet only)](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10169521-projects-in-chatgpt)
- Community consensus (OLD, 2023–2024, flagged as older than 12 months): there is no API to call a custom GPT; "Custom GPTs are only accessible via ChatGPT". — [OpenAI developer forum thread, Oct 2024](https://community.openai.com/t/is-there-still-no-way-to-access-a-custom-gpt-teams-model-by-api/972152)

**Reliability of Actions in 2026 (forum evidence)**
- July 1, 2026: actions intermittently not dispatched; the GPT answers that "the required actions aren't available in this chat session" and the server sees "total silence, not an error"; worse for multi-action GPTs; reports tie it to the GPT-5.6 Sol rollout. OpenAI Support replied on Sept 25, 2026 only that it would "pass this along to our team". Not resolved; related threads continue to Oct 11, 2026. — [OpenAI developer forum, 2026-07-01](https://community.openai.com/t/custom-gpt-actions-intermittently-fail-to-dispatch-actions-not-available-in-this-chat-session-nothing-reaches-our-server/1385448)
- July 13, 2026: a GPT stops reusing an Action's result on the next user turn; behaviour differs by model variant. OpenAI Support (Aug 24, 2026) suggested setting the recommended model to GPT-5.6 Sol, said "we don't have a timeline to share for a fix", and recommended "Plugins, connectors, or MCP-based integrations" for "multi-step interactions or persistent application state." — [OpenAI developer forum, 2026-07-13](https://community.openai.com/t/custom-gpt-does-not-reuse-an-action-response-on-the-next-user-turn-reproducible-after-gpt-5-6-rollout/1386723)

### Inferences
- A custom GPT whose main value is one Action (the reader's case) loses that Action on migration: the instructions and knowledge files survive as a plugin skill, but the link to the reader's web app must be rebuilt, and the documented rebuild route is an MCP server, not OpenAPI.
- Unless the reader's account has an approved deferral (the deferral language is written for Enterprise/FedRAMP), the GPT stops being accessible on December 11, 2026, i.e. about 18 days after these notes.
- The retirement FAQ gives explicit dates only for Enterprise; for Plus/Pro/Business the binding information is the in-product notice in the reader's own account.
- OpenAI itself now steers multi-step or stateful integrations away from Actions (support reply of Aug 24, 2026), so investing further in the Action is not advisable.

### Gaps
- Maximum number of actions/operations per GPT: no current official figure found on the pages opened.
- Whether a GPT's "apps" slot can hold a custom MCP app: the GPT help page does not say; a November 2025 forum question got no staff answer ([forum, 2025-11-20, older](https://community.openai.com/t/support-for-mcp-server-urls-in-custom-gpts/1366998)). With retirement imminent this is moot.
- Exact retirement date and migration options for personal Plus/Pro accounts: not stated in the FAQ; a forum thread asking exactly this (Sept 15, 2026) got a staff reply on Nov 3, 2026 that did not answer it ("Availability can vary by account and device") — [forum, 2026-09-15](https://community.openai.com/t/clarification-needed-custom-gpt-migration-path-for-personal-plus-accounts/1397897).
- What happens to GPT Store links and conversation starters after migration: not mentioned in the FAQ. (Found later in the migration guide, see Addendum point 7: "Conversation starters do not transfer one-to-one." and "At retirement, custom GPTs stop running and leave the GPT directory.")

## 2. Connectors and MCP inside ChatGPT: custom connectors, developer mode, read vs write, plans, admin controls, limitations

### Takeaway
"Connectors" were renamed "apps" (December 2025) and now live inside **plugins** (Plugin Directory since July 9, 2026); a custom connector is now "Add custom MCP server → Create as a plugin", with no developer-mode switch needed. ChatGPT accepts remote MCP servers over HTTPS with OAuth or no authentication (no static API-key option in the documented UI); the Help Center says full read+write MCP is for Business/Enterprise/Edu, Pro is read/fetch only, and it is silent on Plus — while an OpenAI staff member said in June 2026 that "write actions are available to everyone".

### Cited Findings

**Naming and where it lives**
- December 18, 2025 (older than 6 months, kept for the naming history): "connectors now appear in the directory as apps." — [ChatGPT release notes, entry 2025-12-18](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/6825453-chatgpt-release-notes)
- July 9, 2026: "We're also replacing the App Directory with the Plugin Directory." Existing app connections carry over. — [ChatGPT release notes, entry 2026-07-09](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/6825453-chatgpt-release-notes)
- "A plugin brings apps and skills together for a task." An app connects ChatGPT to a service (e.g. Slack); plugins can include skills, connected apps, app templates and extensions. — [Help Center, "Plugins in ChatGPT", updated ~2026-11-22](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001256-plugins-in-chatgpt)

**How a custom MCP server is added**
- Steps: in Plugins "Select the plus button, then **Add custom MCP server**"; enter a name and the Server URL, or select Tunnel (Secure MCP Tunnel); configure authentication; accept the risk warning; "Select **Create as a plugin**"; install the plugin before use. — [OpenAI developer docs, "Add custom MCP server", undated, read 2026-11-23](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/custom-mcp-server.md)
- "You don't need to turn on Developer Mode first." — [Help Center, "Connecting custom MCP servers in ChatGPT", updated ~2026-10-27](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12584461-connecting-custom-mcp-servers-in-chatgpt)
- Transport: "Supported MCP protocols: SSE and streaming HTTP." — [OpenAI developer docs, "Add custom MCP server"](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/custom-mcp-server.md). The endpoint must be "reachable through a public HTTPS endpoint or" a Secure MCP Tunnel, with streamable HTTP "typically at `/mcp`". — [OpenAI developer docs, "Connect to ChatGPT" (plugins), undated](https://developers.openai.com/plugins/deploy/connect-chatgpt)
- Local servers: "Not directly. ChatGPT connects to remote MCP servers."; for private or on-premises servers "use Secure MCP Tunnel". Mobile: "No - web only." — [Help Center, "Connecting custom MCP servers in ChatGPT", updated ~2026-10-27](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12584461-connecting-custom-mcp-servers-in-chatgpt)

**Authentication**
- "Authentication options include **OAuth**, **No authentication**, and **OAuth or no authentication**." (mixed mode: initialize and list-tools without auth, individual tools with OAuth or none). API keys, bearer tokens and custom headers are not listed. For OAuth, "if static credentials are provided, then they will be used." — [OpenAI developer docs, "Add custom MCP server", undated](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/custom-mcp-server.md)
- "If OAuth is configured without **offline_access**, ChatGPT may lose access after the original authorization expires." — [Help Center, "Connecting custom MCP servers in ChatGPT", updated ~2026-10-27](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12584461-connecting-custom-mcp-servers-in-chatgpt)
- Plugin developer guidance: "use OAuth 2.1 when your plugin requires user authentication"; OpenAI-managed mTLS exists "to authenticate ChatGPT as the MCP client"; no bearer/API-key option is described on that page. — [OpenAI developer docs, "MCP server" (plugins), undated](https://developers.openai.com/plugins/build/mcp-server)

**Read versus write**
- "ChatGPT supports both read and write tools from your server." "Write actions by default require confirmation." A tool counts as read-only only with the `readOnlyHint` annotation: "Tools without this hint are treated as write actions." "You can choose to remember the approve or deny choice for a given tool for a conversation." — [OpenAI developer docs, "Add custom MCP server", undated](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/custom-mcp-server.md)
- "ChatGPT can ask for confirmation before important actions or other changes"; "Some especially risky actions may be blocked instead of being presented for approval." — [Help Center, "Connecting custom MCP servers in ChatGPT", updated ~2026-10-27](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12584461-connecting-custom-mcp-servers-in-chatgpt)

**Plans (sources conflict)**
- "Custom MCP server connections and full MCP support are available for ChatGPT Business and Enterprise/Edu customers" (on ChatGPT web). "Full MCP is only available to Business and Enterprise/Edu users, currently." "Pro users can connect custom MCP servers with read/fetch permissions." Free, Go and Plus are not mentioned. — [Help Center, "Connecting custom MCP servers in ChatGPT", updated ~2026-10-27](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12584461-connecting-custom-mcp-servers-in-chatgpt)
- Contradicting statement by an OpenAI staff member on the developer forum, June 23, 2026: "No, write actions are available to everyone." and "Developer mode is not an enterprise level feature, disabling it is." — [OpenAI developer forum, thread opened 2026-06-22](https://community.openai.com/t/write-actions-blocked-on-custom-mcp-server-business-plan-workspace-developer-mode-unavailable-at-workspace-level/1384381)
- A third-party integration guide says Plus and Pro are limited to read/fetch-only custom MCP connectors and write-capable ones need Business/Enterprise/Edu (snippet only, date unknown). — [Boomi documentation](https://help.boomi.com/docs/Atomsphere/Platform/Connect_chatgpt_setup)
- "You can create a plugin with an app hosted on ChatGPT Sites on any ChatGPT plan." — [Help Center, "Plugins in ChatGPT", updated ~2026-11-22](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001256-plugins-in-chatgpt)

**Admin controls**
- Business: "Only admins and owners can create and publish plugins for custom MCP servers for a Business workspace." Enterprise/Edu: "Admins grant permission to create connections to custom MCP servers using **RBAC**." — [Help Center, "Connecting custom MCP servers in ChatGPT", updated ~2026-10-27](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12584461-connecting-custom-mcp-servers-in-chatgpt)
- Workspace permissions: "Use plugins" (on by default for Enterprise), "Upload plugins" (off by default for Enterprise), "Create plugins with MCPs" ("Off by default for Enterprise"), "Share plugins", "Publish plugins to workspace". "New workspace plugins start private." — [Help Center, "Plugins in ChatGPT", updated ~2026-11-22](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001256-plugins-in-chatgpt)
- Tool list is frozen after approval: "After an admin first approves an MCP server connection for the workspace, ChatGPT uses a 'frozen' snapshot of its available tools and inputs." — [Help Center, "Connecting custom MCP servers in ChatGPT", updated ~2026-10-27](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12584461-connecting-custom-mcp-servers-in-chatgpt)
- Business write actions for Google and Microsoft apps: "Write actions remain disabled by default until workspace admins enable them in Workspace settings > Apps > Manage actions." — [ChatGPT Business release notes, entry 2026-03-13](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11391654-chatgpt-business-release-notes)
- "Workspace permissions and security restrictions, including Lockdown, apply to adding and using custom MCP servers." — [OpenAI developer docs, "Add custom MCP server", undated](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/custom-mcp-server.md)

**Where custom MCP works**
- "Deep research can use custom apps, but for read/fetch actions only - not for write actions." "Company knowledge supports custom MCP servers with fetch/search access." "You can use custom MCP servers and plugin MCP servers in the same conversation, subject to account and workspace policies." Dedicated `search`/`fetch` tools "are no longer required." — [Help Center, "Connecting custom MCP servers in ChatGPT", updated ~2026-10-27](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12584461-connecting-custom-mcp-servers-in-chatgpt)
- Workspace agents can use apps "plus your own custom MCPs". — [Help Center, "Workspace agents in ChatGPT", updated Oct 2026](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001143-workspace-agents-in-chatgpt)

**Known limitations and breakages**
- June–July 2026: write (and some read) tool calls to custom MCP servers were rejected before reaching the server with "This tool call was blocked by OpenAI's safety checks. Please double check what you are sending."; one production domain was blocked while an identical test domain worked; partial workaround was rewriting tool descriptions and republishing; "Business workspaces cannot update a published app's tool descriptions in place"; no root-cause fix confirmed in the thread. A Plus user reported the same blocks on June 28, 2026. — [OpenAI developer forum, 2026-06-22 to 2026-07-10](https://community.openai.com/t/write-actions-blocked-on-custom-mcp-server-business-plan-workspace-developer-mode-unavailable-at-workspace-level/1384381)
- Security warning: an untrusted server could access shared information or trick ChatGPT into misusing tools; "Only connect to MCP servers you trust." — [OpenAI developer docs, "Add custom MCP server", undated](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/custom-mcp-server.md)

### Inferences
- For the reader, replacing the Action means putting an MCP front door on the existing web app (the two verbs become two MCP tools: "query" marked read-only, "request" a write tool that asks for confirmation).
- The API-key protection used today has no documented equivalent in ChatGPT's custom-MCP screen (only OAuth, none, or mixed). The web app would need an OAuth layer, or a no-auth endpoint with other protection, or a proxy; this is the single biggest technical change.
- If the Help Center plan table is right, a personal Pro account could only use the "query" side; the "request" side (a write) would need a Business workspace. If the June staff statement is right, it works on any plan. This must be tested in the reader's own account before deciding.
- Because Business admins must publish custom-MCP plugins and the tool list is frozen at approval, any later change to the web app's tools requires republishing.

### Gaps
- Whether Plus (or Go/Free) accounts can add a custom MCP server at all in November 2026: no OpenAI page opened states it; sources conflict.
- No documented numeric limits for MCP tool calls from ChatGPT (timeout, response size, number of tools) were found; the developer pages give none.
- Whether a static API key/bearer token can be stored for a custom MCP server in ChatGPT web: not documented; see the unresolved forum case in question 9.

## 3. Apps in ChatGPT and the Apps SDK: what they are and whether they help agent-to-agent coordination

### Takeaway
The "apps in ChatGPT / Apps SDK" line has been folded into **plugins**: a plugin is skills plus an optional MCP server plus optional UI, published in one directory shared by ChatGPT and Codex. Plugins are a way for ChatGPT to use an outside service (and, since September 29, 2026, for that service to wake a ChatGPT task through MCP events); they are not a channel through which another vendor's agent can address a ChatGPT agent.

### Cited Findings
- "Plugins are the packages people discover, install, share, and publish in ChatGPT and Codex." "ChatGPT and Codex share one universal plugin directory." A skill is "a folder containing a `SKILL.md` file and, when needed, supporting scripts, references, templates, or assets." "Build an MCP server when your plugin must connect to a service". "ChatGPT supports the open MCP Apps UI standard." — [OpenAI developer docs, "Plugin architecture", undated, read 2026-11-23](https://developers.openai.com/plugins/concepts/plugins.md)
- The old Apps SDK address now serves the plugins documentation: the page describes its scope as "Build and publish plugins with skills, MCP servers, and optional UI" and keeps only a reference to "the legacy window.openai APIs". — [developers.openai.com/apps-sdk, read 2026-11-23](https://developers.openai.com/apps-sdk)
- The Help Center still says "Pro users can build apps using the Apps SDK." — [Help Center, "Connecting custom MCP servers in ChatGPT", updated ~2026-10-27](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12584461-connecting-custom-mcp-servers-in-chatgpt)
- March 26, 2026: "Plugins can package skills, optional app integrations, and MCP server configurations in a single place." (plugins first appeared in Codex). — [ChatGPT Business release notes, entry 2026-03-26](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11391654-chatgpt-business-release-notes)
- DevDay, September 29, 2026: plugin extensions ("Plugin extensions let you give your plugin a home in the sidebar."), Plugin Creator, new submission flow and ranking — listed as available on all plans; "Sites can host plugins" for Business, Enterprise, Healthcare, Edu. — [OpenAI, "DevDay 2026 Recap", 2026-09-29](https://openai.com/index/devday-2026-recap/); [TechCrunch, 2026-09-29](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/29/openai-expands-chatgpts-plugins-with-app-like-interfaces-and-automations/)
- "Plugins can now include interactive panels, sidebar views, and viewers or editors for supported file types." — [ChatGPT release notes, entry 2026-09-29](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/6825453-chatgpt-release-notes)
- Plugin Creator: "install Plugin Creator from the plugin directory", then start "a chat with @plugin-creator and describe what you want to build." — [Help Center, "Plugins in ChatGPT", updated ~2026-11-22](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001256-plugins-in-chatgpt)
- Sharing: Business and Enterprise users can share plugins with workspace members; "Publishing to a workspace directory does not publish the plugin to the public directory."; "OpenAI Verified" plugins are reviewed, but the badge "does not replace your organization's privacy, security, or vendor review." — [Help Center, "Plugins in ChatGPT", updated ~2026-11-22](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001256-plugins-in-chatgpt)
- Public publication requires "a stable, publicly reachable HTTPS endpoint"; "Published plugins use continuous review for tool updates." — [OpenAI developer docs, "MCP server" (plugins)](https://developers.openai.com/plugins/build/mcp-server); [OpenAI developer docs, "Connect to ChatGPT"](https://developers.openai.com/plugins/deploy/connect-chatgpt)
- MCP events (inbound signal through a plugin): "Supported plugins can use MCP events to start an automation when something changes in a connected app." — [ChatGPT release notes, entry 2026-09-29](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/6825453-chatgpt-release-notes). Mechanics: "ChatGPT subscribes through your MCP server and supplies a callback URL and signing secret." "Your server sends matching events to that URL." "ChatGPT receives the event in the subscribed chat and follows the user's instructions for how to respond." "Users choose what to monitor and what ChatGPT should do when an update arrives." Limit: one event per request, body "no larger than 256 KiB". Requirements: "ChatGPT supports MCP Events over Streamable HTTP using `initialize`, or over MCP 2.0 HTTP (protocol version `2026-07-28`)"; the server needs persistent subscription storage and outbound HTTPS. Availability: "MCP Events is available in Work chats on ChatGPT web", desktop Work chats with Cloud selected, and with dots. "Polling, streaming, and the draft's `gap` and `terminated` control notifications are not supported." — [OpenAI developer docs, "MCP Events", undated, read 2026-11-23](https://developers.openai.com/plugins/build/mcp-events.md)
- The events specification is a proposal, not part of the official MCP standard: OpenAI's link points to the MCP Triggers and Events working group, where the item is still at an early stage (snippet only). — [WorkOS blog, 2026](https://workos.com/blog/mcp-events-chatgpt-subscription-revocation); OpenAI's own wording is "support for the proposed MCP Events specification" — [TechCrunch, 2026-09-29](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/29/openai-expands-chatgpts-plugins-with-app-like-interfaces-and-automations/)
- Dots "can use plugins to connect to over 4,000 apps." — [OpenAI, "Introducing dots", 2026-09-29](https://openai.com/index/introducing-dots/)

### Inferences
- A plugin with an MCP server is the direct successor of "custom GPT + Action": the skill holds the instructions, the MCP server holds the two verbs.
- Plugins help cross-vendor coordination only indirectly: the same MCP server (the reader's web app) can be connected to ChatGPT and to other assistants that accept remote MCP servers, so the web app becomes the meeting point. The plugin does not make ChatGPT addressable by other agents.
- MCP events are the first documented way for the reader's own system to wake ChatGPT without a person opening it, but they rely on a draft specification and run only in Work chats/dots, so they are an early-adopter option.

### Gaps
- No OpenAI statement was found that formally declares the Apps SDK renamed or retired; the conclusion rests on the documentation layout.
- Plan eligibility for building private plugins with MCP on personal accounts is not stated on the plugin pages.

## 5. The developer side: Responses API, Agents SDK, Agents API, AgentKit/Agent Builder, background mode and webhooks, Codex cloud; rebuilding a GPT as an API-driven agent

## 6. Inbound paths in practice: how Claude, Microsoft Copilot, Grok or a custom orchestrator can hand a task to a ChatGPT-based agent and get the answer back; what is impossible

### Takeaway
There is no way to send a task to a custom GPT from outside: a person must open it. The real inbound doors on the OpenAI side are (a) the **workspace agent API trigger** (Business/Enterprise/Edu; the call is accepted but the answer cannot be read back through the API), (b) **event-triggered tasks** in ChatGPT Work (a new email, Slack channel message or GitHub pull-request event wakes ChatGPT), (c) **MCP events** from your own server, (d) hourly **scheduled tasks** that poll a shared store, and (e) the **developer APIs**, where the caller gets the answer directly. In every ChatGPT-side path the result returns only if the agent itself writes it somewhere the other assistant can read.

### Cited Findings

**Impossible or unsupported today**
- A custom GPT cannot be embedded or called from outside: GPTs "are not a way to embed ChatGPT in an external website or application." — [Help Center, "GPTs in ChatGPT", updated ~2026-11-23](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8554407-gpts-in-chatgpt)
- A custom GPT cannot be scheduled or event-triggered: "Scheduled tasks do not support voice chats, GPTs, or locked chats." — [Help Center, "Scheduled tasks in ChatGPT", updated ~2026-10-31](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10291617)
- A workspace agent's answer cannot be fetched by the caller: "The agent's response cannot currently be retrieved through the API." — [OpenAI developer docs, "Trigger workspace agent runs", undated, read 2026-11-23](https://developers.openai.com/workspace-agents/trigger-runs.md)
- A dot has no mailbox of its own and cannot place calls: "At launch, you cannot give your dot its own standalone email address."; "Your dot cannot initiate calls to you at launch."; texting "works one-on-one between you and your dot." — [Help Center, dots article, updated ~2026-11-22](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001530)
- ChatGPT's own memory is not reachable from other assistants: an independent review says external memory servers "are able to connect to Claude or ChatGPT but not to their memory profiles" and "A conversation with Claude will never be seen by ChatGPT, and vice versa". ChatGPT processes the event asynchronously." — [OpenAI developer docs, "MCP Events"](https://developers.openai.com/plugins/build/mcp-events.md)

**Door E — polling a shared task store**
- A paid-plan scheduled task can run up to hourly, can use plugins and skills when created in Work, and can notify by push or email. — [Help Center, "Scheduled tasks in ChatGPT", updated ~2026-10-31](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10291617); [ChatGPT docs, "Scheduled tasks"](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/automations.md?surface=app)

**Door F — developer APIs (caller gets the answer)**
- Responses API with `background: true`, then webhook `response.completed` or polling by response ID. — [OpenAI developer docs, "Background mode"](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/background)
- Agents API sessions: send a task, then "Stream output or use webhooks to learn when the agent finishes or needs input." — [OpenAI developer docs, "Agents API overview"](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/agents-api/overview)

**Automation tools as go-betweens**
- Zapier offers a webhook trigger ("Catch Hook") that can start "ChatGPT (OpenAI)" actions; Make offers an instant custom-webhook trigger with OpenAI modules (undated product pages, snippets only). These call OpenAI models through the API, not a custom GPT. — [Zapier](https://zapier.com/apps/webhook/integrations/openai); [Make](https://www.make.com/en/integrations/openai-gpt-3/gateway)

### Inferences
- The trigger call for a workspace agent is an ordinary authenticated web request, so anything that can make one (another vendor's agent with an HTTP tool, Power Automate, Zapier, Make, n8n, or the reader's own web app) can start a run. Because the answer cannot be read back, the agent's instructions must end with "write the result to X" — for the reader, X is naturally the web app's "request" verb through MCP, or a Slack channel or shared document.
- The pattern that works across all assistants without OpenAI-specific plumbing is a shared task store (the reader's web app): others post tasks with "request"; ChatGPT picks them up when a person opens it, or hourly through a scheduled task, or immediately through an event; ChatGPT posts results back with "request"; others read with "query". The cost is latency (up to an hour when polling) and occasional approval stops.
- The email door is the most vendor-neutral inbound trigger: any assistant that can send an email can wake a ChatGPT Work task filtered by sender or subject. Replies from ChatGPT may wait for approval.
- Slack is a workable shared room only if the other vendors' agents can also post there and if ChatGPT reacts to their messages; this second condition is not confirmed (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- Whether messages posted in Slack or Teams by bots or other agents (not people) trigger @ChatGPT or a workspace agent is not documented on the pages opened; bot-to-bot loops are often suppressed by design. Needs a live test. For dots it is documented (see Addendum point 3): "Messages from other people, including Slack mentions, do not start work or give them control over the dot."; only the owner or an automation the owner set up starts work.
- Whether Microsoft Teams is a supported channel for workspace agents (only Slack is documented); dots mention Teams in the launch post but the docs page lists only Slack.
- Rate limits and plan limits of the workspace agent trigger API are not documented.
- No source was found describing a tested end-to-end hand-off from Claude, Microsoft 365 Copilot or Grok into a ChatGPT workspace agent; the paths above are assembled from OpenAI's documentation, not from published cross-vendor case studies.
- A third-party blog claims ChatGPT Work subagents can be called via API from n8n and Make; this could not be verified against OpenAI documentation and is excluded.

## 9. Recipes practitioners publish for a custom GPT with an external shared memory or task hub, and the problems they report

### Takeaway
The common recipe is exactly the reader's: a custom GPT whose Action calls a small web service or automation webhook that holds the memory or task list, sometimes with an MCP server in front so Claude and others share it. In 2026 practitioners report three kinds of trouble: Actions that silently fail or forget their own results after model updates, approval prompts and safety blocks that interrupt writes, and — since the retirement notice — no supported way to carry an API-key-protected Action into a plugin.

### Cited Findings

**Recipes**
- GPT → Action → automation webhook: one builder ran "Custom GPT → Custom Action → authenticated Make webhook → IONOS email", with the webhook protected by an API key in an HTTP header. — [OpenAI developer forum, 2026-10-04](https://community.openai.com/t/custom-gpt-plugin-migration-removed-critical-action-integration-with-no-supported-credential-replacement/1403291)
- Moving from Actions to MCP: after the mid-2026 regressions a builder "moved to an OpenAI Plugin using MCP, Agent Skills, and MCP Apps UI"; a stateless MCP test passed, with the tool result returned as `structuredContent` and reused on the next turn. — [OpenAI developer forum, thread 2026-07-13, posts of 2026-07-22 and 2026-08-09](https://community.openai.com/t/custom-gpt-does-not-reuse-an-action-response-on-the-next-user-turn-reproducible-after-gpt-5-6-rollout/1386723)
- One memory for several assistants through MCP: a self-hosted memory service behind a small proxy that handles sign-in; "Cloud clients (Claude apps, Claude Code, Codex) connect via MCP through the Worker."; cloud clients "require OAuth 2.1 with Dynamic Client Registration and PKCE" while the self-hosted service only spoke bearer tokens, hence the proxy. — [Hindsight blog, 2026-04-07](https://hindsight.vectorize.io/blog/2026/04/07/one-memory-for-every-ai-tool)
- Commercial shared-memory servers market the same idea: one vendor says its memory server ships to Claude and ChatGPT as a plugin and to other MCP clients directly (vendor claim, snippet only, date unknown). — [Zep blog](https://blog.getzep.com/agent-memory-mcp/)
- Migration guidance from the trade press: start rebuilding actions as an MCP server now rather than waiting for December (snippet only). — [Developers Digest, 2026](https://www.developersdigest.tech/blog/custom-gpt-retirement-migrate-to-plugins)

**Problems reported**
- Actions not sent at all: the GPT says "the required actions aren't available in this chat session" while the server sees nothing; intermittent; worse with several actions; unresolved as of October 2026. — [OpenAI developer forum, 2026-07-01](https://community.openai.com/t/custom-gpt-actions-intermittently-fail-to-dispatch-actions-not-available-in-this-chat-session-nothing-reaches-our-server/1385448)

- Carrying technical keys as visible conversation text to keep state was judged "too fragile for production" by the builder who tried it. — [OpenAI developer forum, 2026-07-13 thread](https://community.openai.com/t/custom-gpt-does-not-reuse-an-action-response-on-the-next-user-turn-reproducible-after-gpt-5-6-rollout/1386723)
- Behaviour changes with each model update; OpenAI Support (Aug 24, 2026): "we don't have a timeline to share for a fix". — [same thread](https://community.openai.com/t/custom-gpt-does-not-reuse-an-action-response-on-the-next-user-turn-reproducible-after-gpt-5-6-rollout/1386723)

- Migration credential gap: after migrating, "The Custom Action did not migrate."; "The Plugin configuration supports an HTTP MCP server and documents `bearer_token_env_var` for bearer authentication", but "the current ChatGPT Plugin Creator provides **no protected interface for the Plugin owner to supply that environment variable/secret**"; the builder rejects putting the key in the URL or package and objects to deploying an extra proxy. OpenAI Support, Nov 18, 2026: "We're looking deeper into the secure authentication blocker and your question about a protected credential interface." Unresolved. — [OpenAI developer forum, 2026-10-04, staff reply 2026-11-18](https://community.openai.com/t/custom-gpt-plugin-migration-removed-critical-action-integration-with-no-supported-credential-replacement/1403291)

- MCP writes blocked by safety checks before reaching the server, inconsistent between domains; partial fix by rewording tool descriptions as non-destructive. — [OpenAI developer forum, 2026-06-22](https://community.openai.com/t/write-actions-blocked-on-custom-mcp-server-business-plan-workspace-developer-mode-unavailable-at-workspace-level/1384381)
- Native memory stays closed: surveyed memory servers "only allow the user to save and view explicitly set memory, disconnected entirely from the users actual chat history". (Publication date inconsistent on the page.) — [Asimov's Addendum (Substack), "The Memory Walled Garden"](https://asimovaddendum.substack.com/p/the-memory-walled-garden)

### Inferences
- The reader's design choice — the web app writes the records and the GPT only asks and queries through one narrow door — is the same shape practitioners converge on, and it ports well: the same two verbs can be offered as an HTTP endpoint (for orchestrators and other vendors' agents) and as MCP tools (for ChatGPT plugins, and other MCP-capable assistants).
- Keeping all state on the web app's side (task identifiers, status, results) rather than relying on ChatGPT to remember an earlier tool result avoids the "forgets the Action result" class of failures; each turn should be able to re-query the hub.
- The unresolved credential gap means the reader should plan for an OAuth-protected MCP endpoint (or a small proxy that adds it) rather than expect the existing API key to work in a ChatGPT plugin.
- A hub used by several assistants needs to tolerate duplicates and retries, because approval pauses, safety blocks and silent non-dispatch all cause repeated or missing calls.

### Gaps
- No published write-up was found of a completed migration from "custom GPT + API-key Action" to "plugin + MCP" on a personal Plus/Pro account as of late November 2026.
- No practitioner report was found of a working task hand-off loop between a ChatGPT plugin/workspace agent and Microsoft 365 Copilot, Claude or Grok through a shared hub; the shared-memory write-ups found cover Claude, Codex and ChatGPT only, and mostly from vendors.
- Reports about confirmation prompts and "Always allow" come from threads whose dates were not verified; current behaviour should be tested.

## Addendum: dots in depth

Added 2026-11-23 after a follow-up from the coordinator. Working premise: the assistant the reader calls by that name is probably an OpenAI **dot** (possibly alongside an older custom GPT), because the reader's material shows a coordinator with workers, thread IDs, raw HTTPS calls with custom headers and an API key, a local folder on a Windows PC, and a worker that looks things up in Outlook on a laptop. A dot is not a custom GPT. ### Inferences
- **Which OpenAI product matches the reader's evidence.** A dot coordinating Work/Codex threads fits all five signs; a custom GPT fits none of them (a GPT has no workers, no threads it can name, no shell, no local files, no laptop).

| Evidence in the reader's material | Documented dot behaviour | Also possible with |
|---|---|---|
| "Coordinator" with "workers" | background agents; Work or Codex tasks it creates and follows up; Codex subagent role literally named `worker` | ChatGPT Work or Codex used directly with subagents |
| Refers to work by thread IDs | cloud threads and local Codex threads have IDs a dot can be given | ChatGPT Work / Codex |
| Raw HTTPS calls with custom headers and an API key | code and shell commands with internet access on the cloud computer, or commands run on a connected computer | ChatGPT Work / Codex |
| Read a local folder on a Windows PC | connected computer through the ChatGPT desktop app (available on Windows); works only while the PC is on with the app open | ChatGPT Work / Codex desktop |
| A worker looks things up in corporate Outlook on a laptop | a local Work or Codex task on a connected laptop, where the corporate sign-in and network live (the cloud computer does not inherit them) | ChatGPT Work with computer use on that laptop |

- The same signs would also appear if the reader were using ChatGPT Work or Codex in the desktop app without a dot. Three checks settle it: a dot has a profile with "Computers" and "Activity", keeps working when every device is off, and (for Pro) shows its own tab in the mobile app. The past tense "used to read a local folder" is consistent with a connected PC that is now offline, closed, or revoked.
- **Consequence for the reader's web app.** If the link to the web app is made by the dot's own HTTPS calls, the December 11 GPT retirement does not break it; only an older custom GPT with an Action would stop. The price of that route is credential hygiene: an API key the dot keeps in its conversation or notes is visible to the model, appears in conversation records, and can be read by any code running on its computer. The documented safer options are a Codex cloud environment with a network secret, or a plugin/MCP connection that carries its own sign-in; a separate, revocable key for the dot is prudent either way.
- **Consequence for hand-offs from other assistants.** A dot cannot be called. Another vendor's assistant can reach it only indirectly: by writing to a place the owner has told the dot to watch (a filtered Gmail message, a Slack channel, the reader's task hub polled on a schedule or signalled through an MCP event). A Slack mention written by someone or something else does not start work by itself. Results come back the same way: the dot writes to the hub, a file, a channel or an email, subject to its write approvals.
- A dot is personal (one owner, the owner's connections). For a shared, callable agent OpenAI's product is the workspace agent (API trigger), not the dot; specialist dots with their own identity are still enterprise pilots.
- Because a dot's notes cannot be inspected or edited one by one, facts that other assistants must share should live in the external hub, not in the dot's memory.
